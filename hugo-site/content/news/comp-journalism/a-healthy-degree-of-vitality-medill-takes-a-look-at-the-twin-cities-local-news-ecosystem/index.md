This year’s State of Local News Report from Medill was

[grim](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/from-bad-to-worse-2026-is-shaping-up-to-be-a-super-el-nino-year-for-local-news/)
. But in the Twin Cities, Neal St. Anthony
[writes](https://localnewsinitiative.northwestern.edu/posts/2026/11/17/local-news-minneapolis/index.html)
, a rich local news ecosystem offers a more hopeful picture. The
[metro area](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/03/news-diaries-how-a-minnesota-mom-and-minister-blew-past-her-screen-time-limits-when-ice-came-to-her-city/)
of more than 3 million people is home to The Minnesota Star Tribune, public radio powerhouse MPR, local TV stations, and multiple successful nonprofit startups, from nearly two-decade-old MinnPost to
[Sahan Journal](https://www.niemanlab.org/2024/03/how-sahan-journal-grew-into-a-vital-source-of-news-and-information-for-minnesotas-immigrant-communities/)
(whose founder, Mukhtar Ibrahim, also launched
[The Somali Brief](https://www.thesomalibrief.com/)
this fall). Axios has published an Axios Local newsletter for the Twin Cities since 2021, and
[this summer](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/07/the-new-york-times-will-launch-a-local-newsletter-pilot-in-the-twin-cities-next-month/)
, The New York Times launched its own Twin Cities local newsletter.

The question this ecosystem raises, according to St. Anthony, is “whether the Twin Cities represents the potential future of local news in metros across the country: a robust ecosystem with no dominant mass-market player but more news outlets tailored to smaller, niche audiences with narrower but deep interests.” (That description brings to mind

[other urban](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/11/more-of-everything-in-los-angeles-an-explosion-of-new-efforts-aim-to-up-the-citys-local-news-game/)
[media markets](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/03/with-washington-post-local-diminished-other-news-sites-step-up-their-d-c-coverage/)
where a diffuse ecosystem of smaller, newer players have stepped up as legacy media pulls back.)

“There is a healthy degree of vitality in local news in the Twin Cities. The story elsewhere is ‘news deserts’ and little original journalism,” University of Minnesota professor
[Benjamin Toff](https://www.benjamintoff.com/)
told St. Anthony. While local institutions like the Star Tribune have survived, he added, “it’s not immune to industry forces, including the veritable disappearance of print.”

A few highlights from St. Anthony’s piece for Medill:

On the Star Tribune
: The Strib had about 107,000 digital subscribers as of August, up 30% since 2021, while daily print circulation fell 65% over that period, to 57,000. “Our digital subscription business has grown 25% since 2024. We’re trying things. Some will work. We must diversify our revenue streams, including philanthropy,” CEO Steve Grove told St. Anthony. The Star Tribune
[is exploring converting to a nonprofit](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/06/the-minnesota-star-tribune-will-cut-15-of-its-staff-and-may-become-a-nonprofit/)
. A fund launched at the Minneapolis Foundation in 2024 in support of the Star Tribune has raised about $1 million to date. St. Anthony reports the Strib’s sponsored content initiative has attracted more than 60 advertisers. Meanwhile, it’s offering subscribers a free 12-month subscription to The New York Times.

On MPR
: After the 2025 rescission of federal funding for public media, Minnesota Public Radio lost about $6 million. But MPR, like other public broadcasters, was able to turn the rescission into a highly successful fundraising campaign. Its 2026 fiscal revenue rose 5% to $119 million, and it grew to 172,000 paying members, a record.

On Axios Local
: Holly Moore, executive editor of Axios Local, called Twin Cities a “successful market.” She told St. Anthony Axios Local has about 2.5 million free subscribers across its 43 markets, adding, “We want to create a sustainable business model. We are largely ad sales.”
