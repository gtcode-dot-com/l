Canada’s proposed federal budget includes some
[good news for local journalism](https://kitchener.citynews.ca/2026/11/05/ottawa-boosts-journalism-tax-credit-by-501m-extends-eligibility-to-broadcasters/)
. The federal government is extending its journalism tax credit by $501 million, expanding eligibility to audio and audiovisual news organizations, and renewing the
[Local Journalism Initiative](https://www.canada.ca/en/canadian-heritage/services/funding/local-journalism-initiative.html)
, which supports journalism in underserved communities.

Sarah

[wrote about](https://www.niemanlab.org/2020/05/in-canada-a-government-program-to-support-local-news-tries-to-determine-whos-most-deserving/)
the Local Journalism Initiative in its early days. A study earlier this year
[found](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/a-study-of-nonprofit-newsrooms-reveals-some-cracks-in-the-models-foundation/)
that reporters paid with LJI funding believed they maintained their roles as watchdogs and wrote balanced stories, though two-thirds of interviewees were concerned about the perception of a conflict of interest. A growing number of journalists say the benefits of government subsidies for journalism outweigh the drawbacks.

A
[recent report](https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/11/06/How-Can-We-Save-Local-Journalism/)
in The Tyee suggests government support is essential to the survival of local media in Canada. (The Tyee has
[received support](https://thetyee.ca/about_us/)
from the Local Journalism Initiative.)

“It’s either this or death, frankly,” LJI program director and Canadian Association of Journalists president
[Brent Jolly](https://ca.linkedin.com/in/brent-jolly-94389a38)
told The Tyee. “It’s not about local news editors getting their marching orders from the Prime Minister’s Office like some conspiracy theorists want you to think. It’s about infrastructure.”

[Lisa Sygutek](https://ca.linkedin.com/in/lisa-sygutek-34b49710)
, publisher of the
[Crowsnest Pass Herald](https://passherald.ca/)
in Alberta, credited the LJI with growing the publication from “a 16-page paper to a 24-page paper.”
