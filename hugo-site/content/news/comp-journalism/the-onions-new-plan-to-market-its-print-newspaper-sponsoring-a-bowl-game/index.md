[Orange Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Bowl)

[Famous Idaho Potato Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famous_Idaho_Potato_Bowl)

For those unfamiliar, American college football seasons traditionally end with a few dozen bowl games, scattered around the country (and occasionally
[outside it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahamas_Bowl)
). Nearly all of these games sell naming rights to one company or another, leading to fun combinations like the Poulan Weed Eater Independence Bowl, the TaxSlayer.com Gator Bowl, and the Union Home Mortgage Gasparilla Bowl. Collins wants to buy the rights to one of the several bowl games that are still without a sponsor for their games next month.

This absurdity — though no more absurd, I suppose, than the
[Snoop Dogg Arizona Bowl presented by Gin &amp; Juice by Dre and Snoop](https://thearizonabowl.com/game/)
— serves as the excellent marketing hook to get people to buy a
[$75/year print subscription](https://membership.theonion.com/sports/?campaign=701cx000014wVgZAAU)
, a major driver of The Onion’s revenue.

“We are a just a few thousand subscribers away from hitting 100,000 subscribers, which would catapult us above Jeff Bezos’ Washington Post in print circulation,”
[Collins wrote](https://bsky.app/profile/bencollins.bsky.social/post/3mzky7oewq22r)
. “This would make us the 4th biggest newspaper in the United States. So we need more people to sign up before Christmas.”

Indeed, only three U.S. newspapers have a daily print circulation over 100,000 — The Wall Street Journal, The New York Times, and the New York Post; The Washington Post and USA Today have already fallen below that line. (At the risk of being pedantic, though, The Onion appears in print monthly, not daily, making it a bit of an apples-to-oranges comparison. Also, The Onion gets to make up its stories.)

Collins says The Onion actually got quite close to buying the naming rights to the New Orleans Bowl, which last year lost its longtime naming sponsor, the…Ohio-based freight-shipping concern
[R+L Carriers](https://www.rlcarriers.com/)
? The Onion was prepared to pay “mid six figures.” But the organization that runs the bowl, the
[Greater New Orleans Sports Foundation](https://www.gnosports.com/)
, apparently said no deep into the process, a move Collins attributed to “politics.” (I reached out to the Greater New Orleans Sports Foundation for comment — a sentence I did not expect to type today — but did not hear back.)
