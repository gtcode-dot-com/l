Whether on social media or in real life, you might have heard someone — perhaps even political candidates this midterm cycle — talk about data centers. Some welcome them, others shun them.

So, what are these facilities?

They help power everyday tasks such as streaming shows, using Apple Pay, social media or asking a chatbot questions. These activities
[rely on computers and networks](https://www.forbes.com/sites/technology/article/what-is-a-data-center/)
housed in data centers.

While data centers are not new, growing numbers of them have been built around the country in the last few years, increasing locals’ concerns about the sites
[raising](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2026/jul/14/donald-trump/ai-data-centers-electricty-grid/)
electric bills and depleting water sources. Some hopefuls for elected office
[have](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2026/may/07/liz-krueger/electricity-data-centers-new-york-households/)
[called](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2026/jun/12/elizabeth-warren/data-centers-rising-electricity-costs/)
for regulations while others have boasted about the potential
[economic boon](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2026/jul/09/paul-renner/florida-ai-data-centers-tax-breaksgovernors-race/)
.

President Donald Trump said he wants the U.S. to have more data centers,
[including by leasing federally owned land](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/accelerating-federal-permitting-of-data-center-infrastructure/)
. His administration is
[easing](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/accelerating-federal-permitting-of-data-center-infrastructure/)
federal regulations to accelerate approval processes and the construction of new infrastructure to power those facilities.

Here, we’ll explain: what data centers are, how they operate, what artificial intelligence’s role is, and what it could mean for communities when a data center opens nearby.

## **Data centers: An overview**

The term “
[data center](https://datacenters.microsoft.com/whatisadatacenter/)
” generally refers to the physical building that houses powerful computers (usually thousands of them), data storage systems and networking equipment. These facilities are active 24/7,
[storing and managing](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/data-centers)
the data associated with different web applications and services.

“The machines stay on and stay busy,” said Dimitrios Nikolopoulos, a Virginia Tech engineering professor.

The average data center is
[about](https://rpa.org/news/lab/the-rise-of-data-centers)
100,000 square feet, comparable to the average
[Walmart Supercenter](https://www.tastingtable.com/2008091/largest-walmart-us-albany/)
or
[Costco warehouse](https://www.britannica.com/money/Costco)
.

Data centers have been around for
[decades](https://www.enconnex.com/data-center-history-and-evolution/)
; the first one in 1945 housed the
[world’s first programmable computer](https://www.xyzreality.com/resources/evolution-and-future-of-data-centers)
designed to calculate U.S. Army artillery fire during World War II.

Early data centers ran databases, email, and web hosting, using a modest amount of power that varied throughout the day, Nikolopoulos said. Today’s data centers use considerably more electricity because of the larger amount of information they process, he said.

## **What do data centers need to function?**

At their most basic, the computers in these centers need electricity to operate and cooling systems that use water to keep the equipment from overheating.

A typical AI data center can
[consume](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary)
as much electricity annually as 100,000 households; the largest data centers under construction will demand 20 times that amount, the International Energy Agency
[estimated](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai)
in 2025.

In 2024, data centers worldwide consumed about
[415 terawatt hours](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai)
, or about 1.5% of the
[world’s total electricity use](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/global-energy-demands-within-the-ai-regulatory-landscape/)
, according to the International Energy Agency.

The Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, a federal research center, in a June
[report](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/33m6w3x0)
said that by 2030, the total power demand for data centers would account for 9.5% to 15.3% of that year’s total U.S. electricity consumption.

The facilities
[use](https://foe.org/blog/why-do-data-centers-need-fresh-water/)
freshwater, usually from local municipal water systems, to cool the computers. (Other types of water, such as saltwater, could clog and damage equipment as the water evaporates and leaves behind salt and other mineral deposits).

The centers have large air conditioning units, fans and chilled water pipes that cool the computers, which generate a lot of heat while continuously running.

## **The link between data centers and our increasing use of artificial intelligence**

Artificial intelligence
[allows](https://setr.stanford.edu/technology/artificial-intelligence/2026)
computers to perform human-like tasks, including perceiving, reasoning, learning and problem-solving. An
[AI system](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/large-language-models)
trains on
[enormous](https://setr.stanford.edu/technology/artificial-intelligence/2026)
amounts of data.

“Every day digital communications like the internet, banking, healthcare systems, cloud computing/gaming, and websites all use remote servers and therefore data centers,” said via email Ke Xu, a Rochester Institute of Technology professor who develops energy-efficient AI hardware. “AI changed the scale and type of computing, and the intensity normal people use them.”

Xu said a shopping website may require relatively little computer power for each request it gets, but training a large AI model can involve thousands of specialized processors operating together for an extended period. He also said that when large numbers of people send requests to an AI system and expect immediate responses, it requires substantial computing power.

## **How can a new data center affect a community?**

Data center supporters say the facilities can
[create](https://www.wri.org/insights/us-data-center-growth-impacts)
temporary jobs for construction workers and electricians and full-time employment for technicians, security staff and facility managers. The facilities also
[generate](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/turning-the-data-center-boom-into-long-term-local-prosperity/)
revenue for local or state governments through property, sales and use taxes.

A July
[national poll](https://ari.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/26.07.22-Data-Center-National-Polling.pdf)
commissioned by Americans for Responsible Innovation, a nonprofit that advocates for AI policy that protects consumers, found that registered voters were most concerned about the centers’ water use, potentially subsidizing them with tax dollars and paying higher electric bills.

Residential electricity prices have
[risen](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2026/jun/12/elizabeth-warren/data-centers-rising-electricity-costs/)
in the last five years, and data center demand is a big driver in some areas.

[Multiple](https://www.newsweek.com/what-its-really-like-to-live-next-to-a-data-center-12036378)
[news](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/17/us/data-centers-noise-pollution.html)
[outlets](https://www.wxyz.com/news/voices/neighbors-say-noise-from-michigan-data-center-is-24-7-and-upending-their-lives)
have reported that people who live near data centers hear constant noises from the facilities. Residents in
[Virginia](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RAliBRyq_1c)
,
[Michigan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9wDr2dec474)
[and Wisconsin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gc5XZJfF0kQ)
have complained about a persistent hum in their communities. The New York Times
[reported](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/17/us/data-centers-noise-pollution.html)
that some of the noises are ultralow frequency sound waves not audible to humans, but vibrations can still be felt. The Times said some residents reported chronic sleep deprivation, insomnia and other health issues.

## **Where are data centers now and where are they opening?**

There are currently more than 3,000 data centers in the U.S., most in urban areas,
[according](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/04/13/most-new-data-centers-in-the-us-are-coming-to-rural-areas/)
to the Pew Research Center. More than half of planned data centers will be built in rural areas, Pew reported in April.

More than
[1,900 data centers](https://cleanview.co/data-centers/us)
are
[expected to open](https://www.datacentermap.com/usa/texas/dallas/acs-fort-worth-building-b/specs/)
nationwide from now to 2034, hundreds of them in Virginia and Texas.

As of mid-August, some of the
[largest operating data centers](https://cleanview.co/data-centers/us)
are in Texas, Arizona, Wisconsin and Tennessee, according to Cleanview, a market intelligence company that tracks data center development. The
[largest ones](https://rpa.org/news/lab/the-rise-of-data-centers)
are more than one million square feet, about the size
[of 17 football fields](https://www.npr.org/2025/10/14/nx-s1-5565147/google-ai-data-centers-growth-environment-electricity)
.

Amazon, Microsoft, Google, and Meta own data centers across the country, as do data center real estate investment companies that
[lease](https://rpa.org/news/lab/the-rise-of-data-centers)
the space to other organizations.

Companies are
[choosing](https://www.npr.org/2025/10/14/nx-s1-5565147/google-ai-data-centers-growth-environment-electricity)
places such as Virginia and Texas in part because the states offered incentives to build or already had infrastructure that made construction easier or more attractive.

Nikolopoulos said Virginia was one of the first states where
[MAE-EAST](https://clui.org/ludb/site/8100-boone-blvd-data-center)
, the first commercial internet exchange point, physically connected, leaving Northern Virginia with an established higher internet bandwidth. This means that every new building constructed there gets an inexpensive short-distance connection to hundreds of networks, he said.

“Texas competed on different terms,” Nikolopoulos said. “It offered land, tax breaks, plenty of generation and a faster grid connection.”

On Aug. 3, Texas Republican Gov. Greg Abbott
[announced](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/08/03/texas-data-center-project-audit-greg-abbott/)
a pause on data center approvals until state agencies audit how much power and water they will use, whether they are receiving tax breaks and what cooling system they will use.
