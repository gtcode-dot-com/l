# [Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Microsoft will make it easier for publishers to opt out of being scraped](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal/)

Link
[datatracker.ietf.org ↗](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal/)


Posted by
[Laura Hazard Owen](https://www.niemanlab.org/author/lowen/)

[November 2, 2026](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/11/anthropic-openai-google-and-microsoft-will-make-it-easier-for-publishers-to-opt-out-of-being-scraped/)

Right now, if you don’t want your site to be scraped by AI companies, you have to block each company’s crawlers individually. On Friday, Anthropic,
[OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/helping-websites-express-ai-training-preferences/)
,
[Google](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/10/ai-training-control)
, and
[Microsoft](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/10/your-content-your-choice-clearer-preferences-for-training-and-grounding/)
said they would support a
[common robots.txt signal](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal/)
that, per OpenAI, “makes it easier for publishers and website operators to communicate the same preference to multiple participating providers at once.”

Anthropic engineer Kevin Kelley
[proposed](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal/)
the common signal as part of work for the Internet Engineering Task Force, and Anthropic is
[already supporting](https://mailarchive.ietf.org/arch/msg/ai-control/mddjatOxSR0h6GmLRBWlc839c7w/)
it. OpenAI says it will do so “in the coming months,” and Google and Microsoft say they’ll do so next year.

Meta VP of communications Andy Stone
[called](https://x.com/andymstone/status/2116257949773607300?s=20)
the common signal “an important step,” writing in a tweet that the company supports “publishers’ ability to express preferences about how their content is used for foundational generative AI training.” Meta is under scrutiny for proposed commercial terms that would
[allow broad scraping](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the-scraping-of-news-sites-legal-experts-say/)
; last week, it
[agreed](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the-scraping-of-news-sites-legal-experts-say/)
to delay implementation of those terms.
