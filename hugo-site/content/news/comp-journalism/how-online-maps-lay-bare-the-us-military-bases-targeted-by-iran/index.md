In September, CBS News obtained new images of the aftermath of attacks on US bases in the Middle East from active service members. One
[told the news channel](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/new-photos-damage-u-s-kuwait-saudi-arabia-iran-drone-missile-attacks/)
: “This is major damage to our bases that hasn’t been communicated to the American public. “We’re standing there with our eyes closed getting punched in the face,” they added.

While reports have detailed how Iran used
[Russian intelligence](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/03/06/politics/russia-aiding-iran-targeting)
, including
[satellite imagery](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/09/16/world/video/russia-spy-satellites-iran-intelligence-strikes-us-assets-digvid-invs)
, to identify US troops, ships and aircraft, Bellingcat found that publicly available mapping platforms also reveal detailed and potentially sensitive information about US military bases in the Middle East.

Google Maps and other online mapping services contain dozens of labels identifying facilities including headquarters, accommodation, gyms and fuel points. Publicly available photographs and videos, including material published by the US military, provide further details about how these facilities are used.

Experts told Bellingcat that such information could have helped inform Iranian targeting decisions by revealing where personnel gather and how bases operate. However, they cautioned that Iran would also have had access to other sources of intelligence. Reporting from
[Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/pentagon-says-us-military-personnel-are-reportedly-being-targeted-using-location-2026-05-28/)
earlier this year also highlighted how US officials believed commercially available location data was being used to target US troops in war zones.

The bases examined by Bellingcat are not secret and have operated for years in a region where US forces maintained a strong sense of security. Joseph Jarnecki, a research fellow at the
[Royal United Services Institute](https://www.rusi.org/)
, said the US had been able to maintain aerial superiority and was understood to have significant air defence capabilities, but that Iran’s missile and drone campaign has undermined both assumptions.

Iranian strikes have damaged at least
[20 US military sites](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c2l2yl7r8r2o)
across Qatar, Bahrain, the United Arab Emirates (UAE), Jordan, Kuwait, Iraq and Saudi Arabia since the conflict began in February this year.

Governments
[can request](https://transparencyreport.google.com/government-removals/overview?hl=en_GB)
that Google blur or remove sensitive information from its services and Google publishes data on government requests for content removal.

Bellingcat contacted the US Department of Defense who asked us to reach out to CENTCOM about the details in this story. We also contacted Google. However, we did not receive responses to questions from these respondents before publication.

We examined four of the largest and most heavily affected US military installations by Iran’s missile and drone campaign: Camp Buehring and Camp Arifjan in Kuwait, Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan and Al Udeid Air Base in Qatar. At each one, we found publicly available Google Maps information providing details about facilities inside the bases and how they are used.

## Camp Buehring

At Camp Buehring in Kuwait, Google Maps identifies what is labelled as the main headquarters building, as well as a gym and a self-help facility, all of which were among locations destroyed by recent Iranian strikes. In addition to Google Maps place markers there are dozens of reviews and user-uploaded photographs.

Left: Screengrab showing “Camp Beuhring Main HQ, Bldg. 100” Google Maps label. Right: Same building seen destroyed on satellite image published by Iran’s Tasnim news agency.

Bellingcat verified that this was a headquarters building by matching the flagpoles and layout in a photo published by the US military’s
[Defense Visual Information Distribution Service](https://www.dvidshub.net/image/7862187/brigade-support-battalion-area-support-group-kuwait-june-2023)
(DVIDS) showing soldiers posing in front of the  building to the Google Maps satellite image.

## Muwaffaq Salti Air Base

[Three US service members were killed](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/21/us-military-third-service-member-jordan-attack)
in Iranian ballistic missile strikes on Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan on July 17, 2026. The strikes appear to have caused extensive damage to a barracks in the north of the base, with satellite imagery released by Iran showing at least 20 structures destroyed.

Less than 40 metres from the nearest destroyed structure, and within the same living quarters area, Google Maps reveals the location of an “
[Airman’s Attic](https://www.google.com/maps/contrib/115432452417116894880/photos/@31.8324281,36.797149,3a,75y/data=!3m7!1e2!3m5!1sCIHM0ogKEJaX4oqbusDNOQ!2e10!6shttps:%2F%2Flh3.googleusercontent.com%2Fgps-cs%2FAH1DqX9tgf87ld-83XAq_E2g8sxMy6uV12GU0GOLddNmj9LCSvfB7fcgaRF3MDqcys-mkH6hVW2pRpTpwUZSUVwCyCBlJ39iIbihnOFqk3UFh4UT-_l2REPr6FNA6Wh8pHAQr8RL594%3Dw365-h273-k-no!7i3264!8i2448!4m3!8m2!3m1!1e1?entry=ttu&amp;g_ep=EgoyMDI2MDcyNy4wIKXMDSoASAFQAw%3D%3D)

A five-star review posted last year by a soldier includes a ground-level image of the facility, showing uniform drop-off points and fortifications along the perimeter. ## A New Reality in the Middle East

Former US Air Force targeting specialist Wes J. Bryant said the problem facing US bases in the Middle East reflected a broader assumption within the military that bases in allied countries were safe from serious attack. He said the belief that these were rear-area bases where “nothing’s going to happen out here” was a fundamental miscalculation, and that the Google Maps issue was an example of the military failing to account for that vulnerability.

Matthew Savill, Director of Military Sciences at the Royal United Services Institute, said Western militaries do not have consistent policies governing what information can be publicly posted about sensitive sites. He said the issue needs to be considered before a conflict begins, rather than when attacks are already underway. “The start point should be, can we enforce operational security discipline?” Savill pointed to restrictions already in place at some UK military facilities, including Faslane, a naval base that houses the UK’s nuclear submarines, where he said personnel are not permitted to use mobile phones on site.

Savill told Bellingcat that the different pieces of data available on Google Maps, even if outdated, provided a “pattern of life” about US bases in the Middle East. But he added that the use of Google to help with the military targeting of Western bases is not new, saying he encountered the issue while deployed to Iraq more than 20 years ago.

“When I was in Iraq, we knew for a fact that insurgents and militants had been using commercial imagery, including Google Earth, to try and map our bases,” he said. “We have known for more than 20 years that there is a vulnerability that if you don’t stop the collection and layering of data early on, that your bases can be heavily mapped.”

The use of open-source information to support military targeting has been documented in Ukraine. In 2022, Foreign Policy
[detailed how Ukrainian private intelligence company Molfar](https://foreignpolicy.com/2023/03/02/ukraine-russia-war-military-social-media-osint-open-source-intelligence/)
used photographs of Russian soldiers published by Russian media alongside Google Earth satellite imagery to identify their location. Molfar passed its findings to Ukrainian intelligence, and the site was subsequently struck by the Ukrainian military.

The security risks of publicly mapping military installations were recognised by the US military in 2008, when the Pentagon
[banned](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2008/mar/07/google.internet1)
Google’s Street View from documenting the interiors of its bases. Since the Iran war started, US military commanders have also
[warned](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/us-commander-warns-troops-their-videos-help-iran-sources-say-2026-07-29/)
service members about posting photographs and videos from bases after Iranian attacks, cautioning that such footage could help Iran assess the effects of its strikes.

Iranian attacks have
[forced US personnel to evacuate](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/03/25/us/politics/iran-us-bases.html)
many additional locations that were deemed too vulnerable to further strikes.

In total,
[23 US service members have been killed and 865](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/middle_east/2026-10-29/pentagon-iran-war-troops-deaths-23299434.html)
wounded in the US-Iran war, according to
[the Pentagon](https://dcas.dmdc.osd.mil/dcas/conflictCasualties/oo/deaths)
.
