Karoline Leavitt announced last week that she is stepping down as White House press secretary, and a familiar name has emerged as a candidate to replace her.

It’s CNN commentator Scott Jennings.

The polarizing Jennings, who loves to stick up for President Donald Trump while tangling with liberals on “CNN NewsNight,” is an early favorite to replace Leavitt. However, reports state that it’s still early in the process, and that Trump isn’t totally sold on the idea.

[Politico’s Dasha Burns reports](https://x.com/DashaBurns/status/2090437647676199149)
that Jennings is “making a push” to replace Leavitt. Jennings has fiercely defended Trump aide Natalie Harp, whose name has suddenly cropped up in the news. Harp was one of the chosen few to sneak off Air Force One in Turkey recently and onto another plane because of a possible security threat. Georgia Democratic Sen. Jon Ossoff
[said of Trump](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/17/politics/ossoff-natalie-harp-trump)
, “He doesn’t want to do the job. He wants to build his ballroom and travel with Natalie on their apparently defenseless flying palace gifted by the emir of Qatar.”

Jennings responded by firing off several tweets and then going on CNN to defend Harp and Trump. Burns reported, “After clips of Jennings defending Natalie Harp made their way around the WH — including some he sent to senior staffers himself — he reached out to schedule a private meeting with Susie Wiles over the weekend, a person close to the WH told me.”

Wiles is Trump’s chief of staff.

So, does Jennings want the job?

He told Burns, “I don’t have anything to say about that.”

When asked on “CNN NewsNight” by guest host Lulu Garcia-Navarro if he was going to take the job, Jennings said no.

But when CNN’s Kaitlan Collins asked Jennings about it this week, he said, “I don’t really have anything to say about it other than, I mean, I would get to see you every day.”

Collins responded by saying, “That’s not a no.”

But,
[CNN’s Brian Stelter asks the smart question](https://www.cnn.com/newsletters/reliable-sources-08-20-26-105116?utm_source=cnn_Reliable+Sources&amp;utm_medium=email)
: “Would Jennings really want to give up his CNN and Salem Radio salaries for the unforgiving press secretary job?”

It would be a pay cut, one would assume, but it would also put Jennings in the game, so to speak, instead of on the sidelines just talking about it. He worked in the George W. Bush administration, so maybe he would like the chance to get back on the inside.

But does Trump want him?

[NOTUS’s Jasmine Wright and Kadia Goba report](https://www.notus.org/trump-white-house/president-trump-considers-man-white-house-press-secretary)
that Trump is “not quite sold on the idea” of hiring Jennings. However, Wright and Goba add that Trump is interested in hiring a man for the position.

Trump’s first White House press secretary in his first term was a man. Sean Spicer served for six months, but was replaced by Sarah Huckabee Sanders. Two more women served as press secretary in his first term: Stephanie Grisham and then Kayleigh McEnany.

Leavitt has been the only press secretary of his second term. The 28-year-old has decided to step down after recently giving birth to her second child. She will leave the White House at the end of the month.

[The New York Post’s Emily Goodin reported](https://nypost.com/2026/08/20/us-news/heres-why-maga-world-wants-anyone-but-scott-jennings-as-next-white-house-press-secretary/)
Thursday evening that “some of President Trump’s fiercest allies are trying to derail Jennings over his longstanding ties to Sen. Mitch McConnell.”

Jennings has a close friendship with McConnell and once managed McConnell’s re-election campaign.

## A good idea

Longtime fans of “60 Minutes” likely remember the days when Andy Rooney would frequently close the show with a short commentary that ranged from serious political issues to irreverent topics about everyday life. The segment, which ran from 1978 to 2011, showed off Rooney’s curmudgeon and humorous personality, and “A Few Minutes with Andy Rooney” became a fan favorite.

Now, reportedly, “60 Minutes” is considering bringing back something similar to the old Rooney segments. Of course, the program cannot bring back Rooney. He passed away in 2011 at the age of 92.

But some of the names being mentioned as possibilities for the revamped end-of-show segment are interesting.

that CBS News had talked with ESPN personality and podcaster Stephen A. Smith, as well as comedians Jerry Seinfeld and John Cleese from Monty Python. However, Seinfeld reportedly turned down the possibility, and Awful Announcing has since reported that Smith is “unlikely” to join “60 Minutes.”

Meanwhile,
[New York magazine’s Charlotte Klein reported](https://nymag.com/intelligencer/article/bari-weiss-60-minutes-nick-bilton-ross-douthat-cbs-news.html)
that tennis broadcaster (and former tennis star) John McEnroe is a consideration.

The sense is that if the segment does come to fruition, it will have a rotating lineup of commentators rather than a single personality.

I must say, for all the criticism — much of it valid — that CBS News editor-in-chief Bari Weiss and new executive producer Nick Bilton have received for tinkering with a legendary news program, this idea seems inspired. Rooney’s segment was generally well-liked and an updated version with known personalities might add some pizzazz to the show.

And honestly, the names Weiss and Bilton are throwing around are just the kind of interesting personalities who might have something smart and funny to say and could attract viewers.

Who knows if these are the only names being discussed. But if I was putting together a list of possible end-of-show commentators, I’d maybe include Trevor Noah, Marc Maron, Sarah Silverman, Amber Ruffin, Josh Johnson. For a right-leaning perspective, maybe someone like Dennis Miller, “Saturday Night Live’s” former “Weekend Update” anchor.

These are just a few names off the top of my head. But I like what Weiss and Bilton are thinking.

## Sports columnist resigns in protest

A sports columnist for the Seattle Times has resigned in protest after he says the paper refused to run a column that involved the topic of transgender participation in women’s sports.

Matt Calkins, who has been a columnist at the Seattle Times for 11 years,
[wrote on Substack](https://substack.com/home/post/p-212028578)
that the “thesis” of his column was “that supporting the separation of biological females and males doesn’t automatically make you transphobic, so let’s stop throwing those labels around.”

In the column, which you can read
[here](https://mattycee.substack.com/p/agreeing-with-sophie-cunninghams?utm_source=post-email-title&amp;publication_id=10563175&amp;post_id=211906384&amp;utm_campaign=email-post-title&amp;isFreemail=false&amp;r=42vj42&amp;triedRedirect=true&amp;utm_medium=email)
, Calkins wrote from the perspective of two female athletes who competed against trans athletes. In the column, he wrote, “Two things can be true at once. You can show compassion for people who feel trapped in the wrong body while acknowledging they have a biological advantage. After all, why do we separate males and females in sports in the first place?”

Calkins continued on Substack that he turned in a draft of the column, which had not originally been discussed with his editor, on Friday, July 31 and said there was “no rush” to publish it. Calkins then wrote that he was told the column was discussed among newsroom leaders for several days before he was informed it would not run because, he claims, he didn’t tell his editor ahead of time about it.

Calkins wrote that he didn’t buy that excuse and decided to resign, adding “That may come off as an emotional decision, but it was exactly what I was prepared to do.”

He also said this was not the first time he had a column spiked.

He wrote in his Substack, “One was a piece questioning whether players for the U.S. Women’s Soccer Team were really the victims of discrimination in their equal-pay lawsuit. Another was a more general column on cancel culture in sports. The last, written shortly after George Floyd’s death, implored people to listen to state-champion basketball coach Mike Bethea — a black man urging the public not to stereotype cops. The common theme? All of these challenged prevailing newsroom narratives and likely would have triggered the outrage mob.”

Unsurprisingly, conservatives rushed to Calkins’ side.

After announcing his resignation, Calkins appeared on the right-wing podcast of Brandi Kruse. Then, on X, conservative radio and TV host Clay
[tweeted Calkins](https://x.com/ClayTravis/status/2090490264250892423)
to say what happened was “unacceptable” and that he may have a job for Calkins at his new sports media company, Sport &amp; State.
