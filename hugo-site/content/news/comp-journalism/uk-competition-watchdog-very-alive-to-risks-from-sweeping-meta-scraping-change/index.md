***Update 23 October:***
Meta now appears to have delayed the terms and conditions change to 20 November.

News Media Association chief executive Theo Bamber said: “This delay is welcome, but it is a pause, not a solution. As things stand, Meta will still use its dominance to force publishers into an impossible choice: let Meta scrape their websites or lose access to audiences on Facebook.

“We welcome the Competition and Markets Authority’s swift engagement on this issue. The regulator must now secure clear, public commitments from Meta to abandon the offending elements of the new Terms before they take effect.

“If Meta refuses, the CMA must stand ready to begin Strategic Market Status designation, with the binding conduct requirements that follow, and regulators worldwide should be ready to leverage their formal powers. Meta must not be allowed to override robots.txt and help itself to publishers’ content at the price of reaching their own readers.”

***Original story 21 October:***
The UK’s competition watchdog is taking Meta’s plan to implement sweeping new rights for
[AI](https://pressgazette.co.uk/subject/artificial-intelligence/)
access to publisher content via new terms and conditions “very seriously”, its CEO told Parliament.

It emerged at the end of last week that Meta has updated its terms and conditions
[to give itself sweeping rights to scrape content from any publisher or other website owner with a business account on Facebook or Instagram](https://pressgazette.co.uk/news/facebook-terms-publishers/)
and reuse it – even if the content has not been published on a Meta platform.

The new terms are due to come into effect in just over a week, on Friday 30 October. At least one newsbrand has already chosen to leave Facebook and Instagram in response: US local title McKinley Park News.

Competition and Markets Authority executive director of digital markets Will Hayter has written a letter to Meta and had a meeting with the platform set up for today (Wednesday).

But the CMA is unable to take formal action stopping Meta from making the changes because it has not yet designated the tech giant with strategic market status under the Digital Markets Act.

Asked about the change in terms by Conservative peer Veronica Wadley, Baroness Fleet, during a Communications and Digital Committee hearing on Tuesday afternoon, Hayter said: “We’ve been talking to publishers and hearing their concerns, both the public ones and the more private versions…

“We’re talking to Meta about it. We wrote them a letter yesterday to ask them for information on it, and I’m actually talking to them tomorrow to make sure we’ve understood what’s going on, and then we’ll work out what to do next.”

Baroness Fleet, who is a former Evening Standard editor and deputy editor of the Daily Mail and Daily Telegraph, warned that the changes would affect “almost every UK news publisher, and it is clearly uncompetitive leverage of Meta’s strategic market position”.

Hayter responded: “I hear that characterisation. That’s very much the complaint that’s being made from the publisher side.”

CMA chief executive Sarah Cardell added: “We heard of this last week. We engaged directly with Meta. I’ve written to them. Will has a meeting with them tomorrow.

“We share at face value the concerns. We need to understand it better, and we will then think about – this really goes a little bit to the point about informal as well as formal – what steps we can take, both through informal engagement, formal engagement.

“We obviously don’t have a live designation in relation to Meta. There is the option also of using our competition enforcement powers. So we are very alive to the potential impact that this could have for UK businesses, and it is something that we will take very seriously.”

Cardell added that they would “consider urgently” once the CMA understood Meta’s position following Hayter’s meeting.

Campaign group Movement for an Open Web has now filed a formal complaint with the CMA as well as the European Commission’s Directorate General for Competition.

Theo Bamber, chief executive of UK national and local news trade body the News Media Association, urged the CMA to take action.

“Meta’s bid to force publishers to allow unrestricted scraping of their websites to power its AI products is an outrageous assault on journalism.

“The changes would hit UK local publishers hardest as substantial amounts of their referral traffic comes from Facebook. It is vital that publishers have a strong presence there to combat the mis and dis information which plagues Facebook.

“Parliament placed the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act on the statute books to empower the CMA to prevent exactly this kind of cynical and anticompetitive tech platform bullying of UK businesses. The regulator must now use these tools as a matter of urgency.”

Jason Kint, chief executive of US publisher trade body Digital Content Next,
[said he was](https://x.com/jason_kint/status/2112628360908017953)
“impressed that at least the UK is actually moving on this outrageous move by Meta”.

The relevant section in the new Meta terms and conditions states that by using its platforms:

“You authorise us (or any entity acting on our behalf) to use automated software (such as web scrapers, bots, robots, spiders, crawlers and user agents) and create test credentials to access, index, cache, analyse, collect, retrieve or crawl the domains, web pages or URLs (and any content available through them) that are associated with your products or services, or that are made available through your or users’ use of Meta Company Products and services, and to periodically pull updates from such domains, web pages or URLs. Meta may use any content from such domains, web pages or URLs to provide, develop, improve, research, train and maintain the integrity of, the Meta Company Products, services and features provided under these Commercial Terms and any other applicable supplemental terms.”

Meta has since removed the word “train” but US media lawyer Matt Topic
[told Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/legal-experts-warn-metas-new-terms-still-grant-broad-scraping-permissions-despite-language-changes/)
: “The revised version of the new terms still allows Meta to use that scraped content to develop any ‘business product.’ That doesn’t seem to be a defined term, and presumably Meta is developing AI business products. I think ‘develop’ is broad enough to include ‘train.’”

Kint added that the correction was a “farce. It removed ‘train’ but left AI training on the table, promised to honour robots.txt without putting it in its terms, and still claims sweeping rights to scrape websites simply because a random user shares a link.

“Exactly what you’d expect from a gatekeeper notorious for abusing its market power.”

A Meta spokesperson has said the crawling terms “only applied to our ads and business products, and we’ve clarified that in the terms themselves. It does not change how Meta uses web crawlers.

“As always, content owners can use robots.txt to signal their preferences. This is not about scraping news sites, and these updates don’t give us new rights that supersede those preferences.”

[But McKinley Park News editor Justin Kerr said](https://mckinleypark.news/about/letter-from-the-editor/8950-our-local-news-is-leaving-facebook-instagram-and-meta)
: “I don’t trust Meta will do the right thing: It has continually demonstrated bad faith against its members and customers, as well as our economy, society, democracy and journalism.”

He said the local newsbrand would be deleting its Facebook and Instagram accounts because: “Regardless of any benefit, we can neither give away our hard-won, all-original, all-human news product nor expose our users and members to predatory AI scanning and exploitation.

“Although Meta’s new terms do not specify these violations, the terms certainly give Meta the right to commit them without consequence or remuneration.”

Also this week two German news media trade bodies, the German Association of Free Press (MVFP) and the Federal Association of Digital Publishers and Newspaper Publishers (BDZV), called on the European Commission to investigate and stop the T&amp;Cs from coming into effect until it can do so.

They said: “Meta intends to automatically capture, copy, and store the complete websites and archives of publishers and analyse them for its own automated and non-automated commercial products. This makes access to Meta’s platforms contingent on the free surrender of the entire digital publishing product. This is blatant theft. Legally, it constitutes an abusive tying arrangement and a violation of the Digital Markets Act.

“If the EU Commission is serious about preventing at least the worst excesses of the abuse of digital platform power, it must stop this clause before Meta irreversibly creates a fait accompli.”

It noted that the removal of the word “train” did not mean it was prohibited and that training is just one use of AI.
