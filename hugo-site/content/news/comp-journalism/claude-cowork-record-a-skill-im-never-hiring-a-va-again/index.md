## What is Claude Cowork?

VIDEO

Upon first glance, you might think “Claude Cowork” is just a fancier name for the regular Claude chat window.

While they share similar capabilities, the biggest difference is that
[Claude Cowork](https://claude.com/product/cowork)
can actually interact with files and folders on your computer. This allows it to complete multi-step tasks instead of just answering questions.

To sum it up: Claude Chat gives you answers, while Claude Cowork helps you get things done.

### Definition

Claude Cowork is an agentic tool from
[Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/)
that works across your files, folders, and
[connected apps](https://www.unite.ai/best-no-code-app-builders/)
to complete multi-step tasks independently.

That means you’re not typing one question and getting one answer back. You’re handing it a job (like “clean up this messy downloads folder” or “build me a weekly report from these spreadsheets”) and it goes and actually does the work, checking in with you when it hits a decision point.

### Cowork vs. Chat

Hence why it’s called “Cowork.” It feels less like chatting with an AI assistant and more like
[collaborating with a digital coworker](https://www.unite.ai/best-ai-collaboration-tools/)
who can take on tasks alongside you.

That’s the part that tripped me up at first. In a normal Claude conversation, you’re basically doing all the driving. You ask, it answers, you ask again. It’s collaborative, but it’s turn by turn.

Cowork flips that. You describe the outcome you want, and it plans the steps, works through them, and comes back when it’s done or when it needs your input on something ambiguous.

It’s less like chatting with an assistant and more like handing off a task to a coworker and checking back later. Which, I’ll admit, felt weird to trust initially!

### Key Capability: Works Unattended Across Devices

The capability that sold me was how it works unattended in the background. Cowork can keep working even when you’re not sitting there watching it (almost like an employee), and it can pick up across web, desktop, and mobile.

For example, you might kick off a task from your laptop in the morning, get a status update on your phone at lunch, and come back to a finished output that evening.

That shift from constantly prompting an AI to assigning it a task and letting it work in the background is a very different experience from the copy-paste chat loop most of us are used to.

### Real-World Example Use Cases

Here are a few practical Claude Cowork use cases:

* Weekly marketing reports: Pointing Cowork at a folder of campaign data or connected apps and having it compile the numbers into a written report on a schedule, instead of someone manually pulling this together every week.
* Spreadsheet building:
  [Feeding it raw data](https://www.unite.ai/ai-meets-spreadsheets-how-large-language-models-are-getting-better-at-data-analysis/)
  or notes and having it structure, calculate, and format an actual usable sheet.
* Bulk document review: Scanning through a stack of contracts, receipts, or notes and pulling out what matters.

### Record a Skill

You can even
[create custom Skills](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12512198-how-to-create-custom-skills)
by showing Claude how you complete a task, allowing it to learn your process and turn it into a reusable workflow.

Simply
[record yourself completing the task](https://www.unite.ai/best-ai-screen-recording-tools-for-effortless-video-guides/)
, including your screen activity, clicks, and verbal explanation, and Claude will generate a draft Skill based on your workflow. You can then review, refine, and save it for future use.

### Permission Requirements

One important thing worth knowing before diving in: it needs permission before it touches your files.

That permission step might sound annoying, but it’s an important safety layer that gives you control over what Claude can access. Instead of letting an AI freely browse your computer, you decide which folders, files, or actions it can use.

Also, Cowork is only available on the paid plans, and the Record a Skill feature only works on Mac. So if you’re using Claude for free or are on a Windows computer, you’re unfortunately out of luck.

## Claude Cowork Record a Skill Key Features

Here are Claude Cowork’s Record a Skill key features:

* Interactive Visual &amp; Voice Demonstration: Captures your screen activity, mouse movements, typing, and voice explanations to understand both what you do and the reasoning behind your decisions.
* Automated Skill Creation &amp; Suggestions: Turns your recorded workflow into a reusable Skill and can suggest whether to create a new skill or update an existing one.

![Accessing Claude Cowork.](https://www.unite.ai/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Edit-Post-Claude-Cowork-Review-I-Recorded-a-Skill-%E2%80%B9-Unite-AI-%E2%80%94-WordPress-08-07-2026_08_33_AM.png)

![Relaunching Claude to update.](https://www.unite.ai/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Edit-Post-Claude-Cowork-Review-I-Recorded-a-Skill-%E2%80%B9-Unite-AI-%E2%80%94-WordPress-08-07-2026_08_55_AM.png)

Before launching the recorder, make sure Claude is updated to the latest version.

Thankfully, updating is easy. If you see “Relaunch to update” in your Claude window, click it. Claude will update automatically and restart, and you’ll be ready to go.

### Step 4: Go to Record a Skill

![Accessing the Record a skill feature in Claude Cowork.](https://www.unite.ai/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Edit-Post-Claude-Cowork-Review-I-Recorded-a-Skill-%E2%80%B9-Unite-AI-%E2%80%94-WordPress-08-07-2026_08_59_AM.png)

Next, click the “+” (Plus) button on the bottom left of the prompt composer bar, and select “Record a skill.”

### Step 5: Grant Claude Permission

![Starting to record with Claude.](https://www.unite.ai/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Edit-Post-Claude-Cowork-Review-I-Recorded-a-Skill-%E2%80%B9-Unite-AI-%E2%80%94-WordPress-08-07-2026_09_03_AM.png)

Click “Start recording.”

![Granting Claude mouse and keyboard tracking as well as screen visibility before recording a skill.](https://www.unite.ai/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Edit-Post-Claude-Cowork-Review-I-Recorded-a-Skill-%E2%80%B9-Unite-AI-%E2%80%94-WordPress-08-07-2026_09_06_AM.png)

The first time you launch the recorder, macOS will ask for two permissions:

* Accessibility: Allows tracking of mouse clicks and keyboard actions.
* Screen Recording: Gives Claude screen visibility of your application windows (you may need to restart Claude after granting these permissions).

Before granting these permissions, make sure you close private messaging apps, personal files, and browser tabs with private information.

Do not type passwords, API keys, or personal secrets while recording. Everything on your screen and audio feed is captured during the session!

### Step 6: Choose Microphone Settings &amp; Start Recording

![Choosing the microphone settings before recording a skill in Claude Cowork.](https://www.unite.ai/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Edit-Post-Claude-Cowork-Review-I-Recorded-a-Skill-%E2%80%B9-Unite-AI-%E2%80%94-WordPress-08-07-2026_09_32_AM.png)

In the recording preview bar, click the microphone dropdown to select your preferred input mic (or click mute if you prefer to type narration).

Once you’ve granted Claude permissions and have chosen your microphone settings, hit “Start a recording.”

### Step 7: Perform the Workflow

![The Capture Bar when recording a skill with Claude.](https://www.unite.ai/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Edit-Post-Claude-Cowork-Review-I-Recorded-a-Skill-%E2%80%B9-Unite-AI-%E2%80%94-WordPress-08-07-2026_10_07_AM.png)

A floating Capture Bar will appear showing a live timer and active step counter.

* Perform your workflow on-screen at normal speed while narrating your choices into the mic.
* [Sessions can run for up to 10 minutes](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12512198-how-to-create-custom-skills)
  (a 1-minute countdown will warn you before auto-stopping).
* When complete, click the red Done button on the floating Capture Bar. (If you make a mistake, click Discard to cancel without saving).

### Step 8: Finish Recording &amp; Add Connectors

![Claude Cowork Record a Skill feature asking to add connectors.](https://www.unite.ai/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/untitled-08-07-2026_10_21_AM.png)

Once you click Done, Claude processes the recording, discards the raw audio and video files, and saves the key screenshots it needs for context.

It may also ask a few clarifying questions and offer to add connectors for any tools you used, such as Apple Notes or Google Drive. For a more automated workflow, you can connect these tools, choose to handle the task manually, or skip the request altogether.

![Claude providing a sample preview of a skill before providing an option to save the skill or not.](https://www.unite.ai/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Screenshot-2026-08-07-at-9.59.31-AM.png)

Claude may even provide a sample preview of the skill before you choose to save the skill or not.

## Top 3 Claude Cowork Record a Skill Alternatives

Here are the best Claude Cowork Record a Skill alternatives I’d recommend.

The first Claude Cowork “Record a Skill” alternative I’d recommend is OpenAI’s Codex Record &amp; Replay.

It’s OpenAI’s version of showing an AI how to do a task so it can turn that workflow into a reusable skill. Codex lets you demonstrate a workflow on your Mac and turn it into a structured asset, making it a strong alternative if you want to teach an AI a process by showing it rather than writing detailed instructions.

Both tools let you record a desktop workflow and turn it into a reusable skill, but they differ in execution.

On the one hand, Claude Cowork keeps things simple: you record your screen and voice, review the skill it creates, and save it. Meanwhile, Codex Record &amp; Replay turns your recorded actions into editable files that can work with computer controls, browser actions, and connected plugins.

For a simple way to teach Claude your workflow through quick screen recording and narration, choose Claude Cowork Record a Skill. For more control and deeper desktop automation, choose Codex Record &amp; Replay.

However, they take very different approaches. Claude Cowork’s Record a Skill learns your workflow by watching you perform it, making it especially easy to turn your process into a reusable skill. Gumloop is better for more complex automations that can run on their own, connect multiple apps, handle several steps, and be managed across a team.

If you want to teach Claude a repeatable workflow by simply showing it what to do, choose Claude Cowork Record a Skill. If you need more advanced automations that connect multiple apps and can run on their own, Gumloop is the better choice.

The final Claude Cowork Record a Skill alternative I’d recommend is OpenWork. It’s a free, open-source desktop app that lets you use AI agents from more than 50 providers, connect your own API keys, and run tasks across your files and apps.

Both tools can turn repeatable workflows into reusable skills, making them useful for automating everyday computer tasks. However, they take different approaches.

On the on hand, Claude Cowork’s Record a Skill lets you show Claude how you work by recording your screen, mouse movements, typing, and voice. OpenWork is more focused on giving you a flexible AI workspace where you can use different models, connect tools through MCPs, and share skills and setups with your team.

OpenWork also has a major advantage if you don’t want to be locked into one AI provider. It supports models from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and more, while Claude Cowork is built around Claude. OpenWork also works on Mac, Windows, and Linux, while Record a Skill is currently limited to Claude for Mac.

For the easiest way to create a Skill by simply showing Claude your workflow, choose Claude Cowork Record a Skill. For a free, open-source option that works with multiple AI models and operating systems, choose OpenWork.
