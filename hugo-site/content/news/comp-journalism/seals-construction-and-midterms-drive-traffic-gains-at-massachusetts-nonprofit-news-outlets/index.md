Is something in the water in Nieman Lab’s home state? In our latest quarterly ranking of nonprofit news outlets, eight Massachusetts nonprofit news outlets enjoyed significant jumps in traffic. Massachusetts is the state that appeared most frequently in this quarter’s data, followed by California (7), Michigan (4), and New York (4).

Four of those Massachusetts outlets largely attributed the traffic spikes to hyperlocal stories that served specific reader needs and curiosities.

[Cambridge Day](https://www.cambridgeday.com/)
garnered 160,000 visits in July, a 60% increase from June. Editor
[Michael Fitzgerald](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mffitzgerald/)
(a 2011 Nieman Fellow) said the publication’s
[top story](https://www.cambridgeday.com/2026/07/14/city-stops-demolition-begun-without-permits)
that month was about the city halting a multifamily home demolition. Other big stories covered the
[sale of two independent movie theaters](https://www.cambridgeday.com/2026/07/10/somerville-capital-theater-for-sale/)
and a
[July 4 shooting](https://www.cambridgeday.com/2026/07/04/apparent-shooting-death-cambridge/)
.

Reader support allowed Cambridge Day to hire a full-time reporter in June, along with four summer intern reporters whose positions were funded through their universities. In July, Fitzgerald said, the news outlet published 32 stories per week.

“Having full-time reporters was transformative. News does not pay a living wage to freelancers,” Fitzgerald said in an email. “They have to pay their bills by doing other things, so they just aren’t as available as full-time reporters. We made the decision to hire a second reporter, who started in late August. We took a bit of a risk and brought on [a] freelancer as a six-month editorial fellow. I cut my freelance budget in half and have rejiggered some other expenses to make this less risky.”

[The Lexington Observer](https://lexobserver.org/)
got 62,000 visits in August, a 47% increase from the previous month. Executive director
[Lauren Feeney](https://www.linkedin.com/in/lauren-feeney-7828bb3/)
said the
[August story](https://lexobserver.org/2026/08/20/resident-appeal-delays-lexington-school-building-project-and-adds-millions-to-cost/)
with the most traffic was about how a resident-led complaint delayed the construction of what would be the most expensive high school in the country.

Election coverage ahead of the midterms also resonated. “
[Our](https://lexobserver.org/2026/07/30/jonathan-paz-challenges-katherine-clark-for-her-seat-in-congress/)
[coverage](https://lexobserver.org/2026/08/06/tarik-samman-is-running-for-congress/)
of two little-known progressive candidates running to unseat [Rep. Katherine Clark] from the left got a ton of traffic, I believe because hardly anything else had been written about them, so readers came to our site not just from Lexington but from the entire 5th Congressional district, including Cambridge,” Feeney said. “My takeaway from this is to keep focusing on undercovered and hyperlocal stories, and not spend a lot of resources on stories that are already widely covered.”

August was also a newsy month for the
[Marblehead Current](https://marbleheadcurrent.org/)
. With 156 published stories in August, the Current’s traffic increased by 53% compared to July. Cofounder and reporter
[Leigh Blander](https://www.linkedin.com/in/leighblander/)
said her
[investigation](https://marbleheadcurrent.org/2026/08/25/looking-for-answers-to-road-and-sidewalk-projects/)
into the city’s road conditions sparked engagement on Facebook and Instagram, driving pageviews. Other stories that resonated with readers covered a
[boat crash](https://marbleheadcurrent.org/2026/08/07/marblehead-boating-accident-sends-five-to-hospital/)
, a
[seal carcass](https://marbleheadcurrent.org/2026/08/05/seal-carcasses-near-barnacle-show-signs-of-great-white-shark-bites/)
,
[racist graffiti](https://marbleheadcurrent.org/2026/08/18/family-finds-racist-graffiti-carved-into-marblehead-apartment-hallway/)
, and
[education coverage](https://marbleheadcurrent.org/2026/08/26/after-cuts-and-turmoil-superintendent-looks-toward-stable-future-as-classes-start-monday/)
during back-to-school season.

[CommonWealth Beacon](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/)
, which covers statewide politics and policy, saw its September traffic increase by 37% over August. Editor
[Laura Colarusso](https://www.linkedin.com/in/laura-colarusso-2961967/)
attributed the growth to the publication’s coverage of Massachusetts’ nine ballot questions.

“Our
[ballot question guide](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/ballot-question/)
brought a lot of new readers to our site and strengthened our relationship with many of the hyperlocal news organizations that regularly republish our stories,” Colarusso said. “We’ve not made any concrete changes yet, but that experience has led to discussions around what tools we can leverage to create a deeper relationship with people who are coming to our site for the first time.”

Other Massachusetts-based outlets in this quarter’s data included
[The New Bedford Light](https://newbedfordlight.org/)
,
[The Newton Beacon](https://www.newtonbeacon.org/)
, the
[Plymouth Independent](https://www.plymouthindependent.org/)
, and
[The Belmont Voice](https://belmontvoice.org/)
.

Other outlets with notable gains this quarter:

[Votebeat](https://www.votebeat.org/)
, the national nonprofit covering U.S. elections, saw a 154% increase in website visits in July, up from 62,600 visits in June. Growth and community editor
[Lauren Aguirre](https://www.linkedin.com/in/laurencaguirre/)
said that jump was the result of publishing a scoop that
[President Trump had fired all of the members of the Election Assistance Commission](https://www.votebeat.org/national/2026/07/09/trump-fires-election-assistance-commission-members-hicks-hovland-mccormick/)
.

That story alone brought in 110,047 pageviews, and 17% of those came from Reddit.

“With the demise of Twitter, we’ve really been leaning into Reddit and other spaces where political nerds like to hang out,” Aguirre said. “The outsized attention this story received on Reddit is absolutely due to our continued presence over there. Before Votebeat began posting on Reddit a few years ago, we’d almost never be linked in communities like this.”

[Shelterforce](https://shelterforce.org/)
, a nonprofit publication covering affordable housing, saw its July traffic increase by 85%, from 43,100 in June to 79,800. Director of editorial operations
[Lillian Ortiz](https://www.linkedin.com/in/lillianmortiz/)
attributed the spike to a story about
[a federal government proposal that put rental assistance at risk](https://shelterforce.org/2026/07/07/huds-work-requirement-and-time-limit-proposals-put-rental-assistance-at-risk/)
. That story received 18,000 pageviews and is one of the site’s top stories of the year. Ortiz also suspects that more readers are becoming listeners as Shelterforce grows its offering of audio versions of its stories. So far this year, Shelterforce has logged 30,000 listens, a 16% increase over 2025.

“Many of our authors read their stories, and that has helped us reach new audiences and give people another way to engage with our work,” Ortiz said. “While the audio is embedded with stories on our website, people can also listen to us on other audio-streaming platforms. We also offer AI-generated audio for stories that aren’t read by a person.”

[The Buckeye Flame](https://thebuckeyeflame.com/)
, which covers LGBTQ+ issues in Ohio, got 45,000 unique visits in July, up from 25,000 in June. Editor
[Ken Schneck](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kenschneck/)
said that the Flame continued to report on its usual beats, including state politics and rural issues, and no particular story drove a surge in traffic.

“The July traffic is so affirming because we literally changed nothing about how we do what we do,” Schneck said. “Our goal is to publish a new piece every weekday and to amplify topics ranging from policy and legislation to rural coverage to queer joy, and that’s exactly what happened for us in July.”

[The Imprint](https://imprintnews.org/)
, a national nonprofit covering the child welfare and juvenile justice systems, saw an 82% increase in its August traffic over July, from 68,500 to 124,800. Co-executive director
[Christie Renick](https://www.linkedin.com/in/christie-renick-23405757/)
said one story — about the state of Utah
[revoking the license of a mental health treatment facility](https://imprintnews.org/child-welfare-2/utah-pulls-provo-canyon-schools-license-after-yearslong-campaign-by-youth-advocates-and-celebrity-paris-hilton-a-former-resident/275820)
following complaints from advocates including Paris Hilton — drove much of that traffic and resulted in nearly 25,000 pageviews. (Renick couldn’t find any evidence that Hilton herself shared the story.)

The
[Juneau Independent](https://www.juneauindependent.com/)
saw a 40% increase in visits from July to August, and a 31% increase from August to September. In real numbers, the Independent went from 41,800 visits in July to 77,300 by the end of September.

The news of the spike was a surprise to editor
[Mark Sabbatini](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mark-sabbatini-57468b33/)
, who makes up one-fourth of the newsroom that launched in June 2025 to cover Alaska’s capital. Sabbatini attributed the August traffic growth to the Independent’s coverage of a
[glacial lake outburst flood](https://www.juneauindependent.com/post/initial-assessment-of-2026-glacial-lake-outburst-flood-confirms-less-damage-than-last-year)
, “a disaster-level event Juneau has experienced every August for the past four years.”

“We see it as a core responsibility to provide the best possible coverage before, during and after those floods, and our staff worked around the clock to provide updates as flooding peaked and during its aftermath. In addition to high local readership, that coverage is also read, cited, and republished elsewhere,” Sabbatini said. “It seems fair to state the Independent and [public radio station] KTOO are now considered Juneau’s primary news sources and what people were relying on for critical information as an emergency situation unfolded.”
