**Google on Tuesday announced the release of Chrome 158 to the stable channel with patches for 204 vulnerabilities, including six critical-severity bugs.**

The critical flaws include three use-after-free issues in WebView, GarbageCollection, and WebAudio, out-of-bounds write defects in GPU and ANGLE, and a buffer overflow in Media.

No bug bounty reward will be paid for any of these. In fact, no reward will be handed out for 163 of the security weaknesses. Google and Xinyang Ge discovered 145 and 15 of them, respectively.

Of the remaining vulnerabilities, 62 are high-severity issues, 82 are medium-severity, and 54 are low-severity.

Google
[says](https://chromereleases.googleblog.com/2026/11/stable-channel-update-for-desktop_01699533288.html)
it handed out $11,000 in rewards for five of the vulnerabilities reported by external researchers and has yet to determine the amounts to be paid for 36 flaws.

The most common type of vulnerability patched in Chrome 158 is use-after-free (51 occurrences), followed by incorrect authorization (34), missing authorization (16), out-of-bounds write/read (15), uninitialized resource (14), information leak (11), UI misrepresentation (10), improper input validation (9), and race condition (8).

Google makes no mention of any of these vulnerabilities being exploited in the wild, but users are advised to update their browsers as soon as possible.

The latest Chrome iteration is now rolling out as versions 158.0.8127.36/.37 for Windows and macOS, and as version 158.0.8127.36 for Linux.
