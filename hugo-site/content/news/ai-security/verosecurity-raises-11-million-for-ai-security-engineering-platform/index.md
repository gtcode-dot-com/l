**[Vero Security](https://www.vero.security/)
, founded in 2025 by IDF 8200 alumni Elad Tessler (CEO) and Daniel Meirov (CTO), has raised $11 million in a seed funding round led by TLV Partners. The firm is building a platform for AI Security Engineering (ASE) – the process of securing AI tools and agents – and will use the funding to advance the platform and grow its go-to-market presence in the United States.**

AI Security Engineering is a new, and as yet ill-defined, security discipline. Its purpose is to secure the AI tools and agents that are increasingly being adopted, used, and modified in-house by company employees. This is happening at pace and volume – so the object of ASE is a moving target; and is a security requirement that is different to and separate from cybersecurity.

ASE policy is defined by security teams. Vero turns these policy definitions into enforced controls, automatically updates them as the AI tools evolve, and refers only those enforcement decisions that require a human judgement to the security team.

Vero’s idea is to bring a new approach to securing AI. “CISOs have seen enough dashboards and tables that tell their teams what’s wrong and leave the work to them. AI Security Engineering deserves a better operating model, designed for AI rather than borrowed from other areas of security,” commented Elad Tessler, Co-Founder and CEO of Vero Security. “We’re working alongside the first generation of AI security leaders and building Vero around what they actually need: a platform that does the work, keeps protections current as AI changes, and brings them only the decisions that require human judgment.”

The core technology within the platform is Vero’s AI Runtime Governor. It puts security decisions into effect as AI operates, enforcing approved policies and blocking agent actions that trigger unauthorized behavior. If a change requires a new risk decision, Vero brings the analysis and recommended action to the responsible security professional.

In parallel with developing its AI Security Engineering platform, Vero is establishing AISecurityEngineering.org, an open community and knowledge center featuring practical field guides, workshops and the ASE credential track. ASE is new, rapidly growing, and different to existing mainstream cybersecurity.

“Our own weekly count of open roles titled ‘AI Security Engineer’ went from 367 on 25 October to 451 on 14 November, up 23% in three weeks,” Tessler told
*SecurityWeek*
. “Demand is arriving before the discipline has been written down.”

He added, “Security disciplines that have lasted, like AppSec and detection engineering, grew a shared body of knowledge before they had standard job titles. AI Security Engineering needs that too, and right now!”

Vero is bringing order to the evolving and disorganized security requirement of AI Security Engineering. While the new open community will help to define and establish the discipline, Vero’s own platform will assist the ASE role to perform the operational work.

“The Vero platform undertakes the impossible part of the ASE work, leaving the human engineer in situ to make judgement calls where necessary,” explained Tessler. The impossible part is the volume (dozens of tool changes every week, each one mapped to every control they touch), and tempo (agents act in seconds, so defenses must be in place before the action, not reviewed after it) of AI tool changes. “The human part is deciding what the business will accept, which exceptions make sense, and when a new tool is worth the risk.”

“We’re betting on more AI security engineers, not fewer,” said Tessler, “which is why we’re building AISecurityEngineering.org to train them. Without Vero, keeping up with AI in a large company takes a team. With it, one person, or a security leader taking it on alongside their job, can stay ahead.”

Vero is adding flesh to a newly emerging security requirement: AI Security Engineering. The Vero platform and AI Runtime Governor automates much of the mechanics of securing AI tools and agents used in-house, by continuously discovering, assessing and securing enterprise AI. Additionally, launching the separate AISecurityEngineering.org open community will help establish a body of subject knowledge to sustain and grow the new discipline.
