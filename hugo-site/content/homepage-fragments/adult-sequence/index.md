Gene Hartmann told me, “Stay away from [name withheld].” Rita Hartmann added, “Or you will get whacked.” When I reported the threat to my assigned lawyer, Audrey Stanley, she called it a “conditional death threat” and dismissed it as “no big deal.” Years later, Officer Brandt prevented another officer from receiving my report of the same threat.

I name the people involved, describe what I saw and heard, and report what happened when I sought protection or accountability.

## I. Death threats and pressure to stay silent

1. <span id="account-3" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Gene (Eugene) Hartmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Security_Agency) — “Stay away,” then a “murder mystery.”** I knew Gene and Rita Hartmann through the Haleʻiwa duplex where I rented the back unit and they occupied the front. During an in-person encounter after February 2016 and before July 2017, Gene calmly told me, “Stay away from [name withheld].” Rita immediately supplied the consequence: “Or you will get whacked.”

   Gene knew I was investigating and I planned to disclose what I learned at trial. The following day, Gene recast the exchange as a “murder mystery.” I told him I would tell everyone. He and Rita also pressed me to go away or leave Hawaiʻi.

2. <span id="account-4" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**Rita Hartmann — “Or you will get whacked.”** Rita delivered the death threat that completed Gene’s instruction to stay away. She spoke calmly and quietly during the same encounter and also participated in the pressure for me to leave. Her words made the consequence explicit: death if I did not comply.

3. <span id="account-5" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**“Kevin” — A warning to stop talking, backed by a threat to destroy my career.** In May or June 2017, a man I identify as Kevin approached me outside Kahala Whole Foods. He described himself as an associate of the public figure named in the earlier threat and that person’s spouse. He threatened to destroy my career if I continued discussing the Hartmann threat and related conduct.

   Kevin also invoked a prominent technology investor’s name to reinforce the warning. The encounter came shortly before I would testify in July 2017.

## II. The tax-office account, the imaginary gun, and my lawyers

4. <span id="account-1" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Young Kwak](https://tax.hawaii.gov/) — A demand for seven times the tax owed, followed by false accounts of the encounter.** In November 2015, Kwak verbally attacked and aggressively pressured me after the door closed on a tiny, enclosed tax-office booth. He demanded approximately seven times what I owed. I told him the amount I owed; the final calculation came in just below the figure I had given him. He threatened that leaving would mean dealing with someone who “wasn’t nice.”

   Kwak subsequently gave the grand jury a false accusation about what I had said. I heard that accusation in the grand-jury recording. My words concerned exposing what was happening and telling everyone. At trial, the earlier accusation was not presented to the jury. Kwak instead testified, in substance, that he merely told me which form to fill out. That exchange did not happen. His routine-form account left out the aggressive pressure and gave the jury a false picture of his conduct. His description of that encounter was central to what the jury was being asked to believe.

   The diagram presented to the jury made the enclosed booth appear substantially larger and more comfortable than it was. The jury saw that depiction without the photograph of the actual booth I had asked my lawyer to obtain. The form story and the diagram reinforced the same false picture: routine instructions in a roomy setting, rather than aggressive pressure inside a tiny closed booth.

   [![Tax-office cartoon showing a man in a confined booth facing a shouting collector labeled Y. Kwak, beside a checklist of seven times the tax owed, false testimony, and a misleading booth diagram.](/img/homepage-tax-office.jpg)](https://tax.hawaii.gov/)

5. <span id="account-2" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[A man introduced as a New Jersey State Police investigator](https://www.njsp.gov/) — The “colonoscopy” warning.** In an encounter I place in November 2015, after the tax-office dispute, a man identified to me as a New Jersey State Police organized-crime-task-force investigator demonstrated knowledge of my childhood associates and invoked their organized-crime associations. He told me he would give me a “colonoscopy.” James Yuen stood beside him.

   He [invoked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethals_Bridge) [my](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10926771.2020.1774695) [personal](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25197837/) [history](https://www.cdc.gov/aces/about/) while delivering that warning during questioning about the tax-office matter.

6. <span id="account-9" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[James Yuen](https://openpayrolls.com/employee/james-yuen-9557) — Beside the investigator during the threat, then preparing the case with Kanemoto.** Yuen stood beside the man who invoked my childhood associates and delivered the “colonoscopy” warning. Yuen handled the tax investigation. I later personally observed him working directly with prosecutor Vincent Kanemoto in trial preparation; that preparation also involved Kwak.

   What information did Yuen supply to Kanemoto? How did Kwak’s accounts develop, when did the routine-form description first appear, and how was it prepared?

7. <span id="account-6" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Vincent Kanemoto](https://ag.hawaii.gov/) — Pointing an imaginary gun at the jury and urging conviction.** During closing argument in July 2017, Kanemoto acknowledged that no gun was involved. He then formed both hands into a mock pistol, pointed it toward all twelve jurors, and asked them to imagine a gun anyway. His closing words were, in substance: “There was no gun in this trial. But what if there was? Convict him. I rest my case.”

   The judge immediately called a recess and brought the lawyers into chambers. I also recall an instruction to disregard the episode.

   Kanemoto’s presentation omitted Kwak’s earlier false accusation and the recordings of my distress, while putting forward Kwak’s false routine-form account and the misleading booth diagram. Yuen worked directly with him preparing the case. The actual death threat I had reported to my lawyers did not reach the jury through my testimony; Kanemoto’s imaginary weapon did reach the jury through his closing.

   When I confronted Kanemoto at Glazer’s in Haleʻiwa in an encounter I place in summer 2019, he denied making the mock-pistol gesture.

8. <span id="account-7" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Audrey L.E. Stanley](https://lawyers.findlaw.com/hawaii/honolulu/5063522_1/) — Dismissing the death threat and relaying a proposal that I leave Hawaiʻi.** Stanley was my assigned lawyer when I reported the Hartmann threat. I began explaining who had spoken, the condition attached to the threat, and why I took it seriously. She interrupted, called it a “conditional death threat,” and dismissed it, in substance, as “no big deal.” She did not explain how it would be documented, investigated, or addressed, or what reporting and protective options were available.

   Separately, while reviewing the grand-jury account of my distress during the tax-office encounter, she laughed.

   Not long after my threat report, Stanley relayed a proposal originating with the prosecutor that centered on my departure from Hawaiʻi. She supplied no written terms or clear explanation.

   Stanley’s [2024 judicial financial disclosure](https://www.courts.state.hi.us/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/StanleyAudrey2024FDS.pdf#page=1) gives September 23, 2024 as the date of her appointment as a per diem Family Court judge. The conduct described here concerns her earlier representation of me.

9. <span id="account-8" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**Unnamed retained lawyer — The threat left out, the evidence left unchallenged, and the pressure after I said no.**

   **The death-threat report and my testimony.** I personally told him that Gene and Rita Hartmann had threatened to kill me. He did not take it seriously, did not explain investigative or protective options, and advised me to leave the death threat out of my testimony. I followed his advice. The jury did not hear that account from me. Kevin’s warning to stop talking was also still affecting my willingness to disclose the threat. The lawyer’s advice came on top of that pressure.

   **The high-priest connection.** My lawyer described the person at the [2016 Eddie competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eddie) as “the high priest.” His connection to that person is part of my concern about his independence in handling the death threat from Gene and Rita Hartmann.

   **The fees and the additional work.** His stated fees were $6,000 or $8,000, depending on the scope of representation. I paid the additional $2,000 for the fuller proceedings, rather than accepting the resolution he advocated. His agreement specified no compensation for repeating the proceedings. He repeatedly pressed me to accept a resolution despite my refusals. How did that fee arrangement affect his decisions about the available remedies and his advice to me?

   **“Tight trial,” “looking guilty,” and “Maybe he did it.”** Before the proceedings, he told me the judge wanted a “tight trial.” During preparation he said he was counting on me looking guilty. He opened with words to the effect of “Maybe he did it.” He was introducing the possibility of my guilt before testing the witnesses. His pressure to accept a resolution had already begun before the mock-pistol incident; it continued despite my repeated refusals.

   **Kwak’s testimony and my immediate objection.** His cross-examination of Kwak was cursory. I immediately told him that Kwak’s routine-form testimony was false and that the exchange Kwak described had never happened. He dismissed the point, in substance, as immaterial and not perjury. That answer did not address the false picture being given to the jury or Kwak’s credibility. My complaint was about a witness describing an exchange that did not happen. What did counsel do to compare that testimony with Kwak’s earlier accounts, test when the form detail first appeared, and challenge it before the jury?

   **The missing photograph and misleading diagram.** Before the proceedings, I asked him to obtain a photograph of the actual enclosed booth and use it as an exhibit. He did not obtain or use it. The jury saw a diagram that made the booth substantially larger and more comfortable than it was. He did not adequately challenge that depiction. The photograph I requested would have given the jury a direct comparison with the place where the encounter occurred.

   **What he failed to explain after chambers.** My lawyer did not adequately explain what happened in chambers immediately after Kanemoto’s mock-pistol closing, whether the instruction to disregard the episode was sufficient, or the alternatives to continuing before the same jury. No mistrial motion was made in my presence. The questions included an objection, a mistrial request, further corrective instructions, describing the gesture on the record, and preserving the incident for later challenge. A transcript of the prosecutor’s words would not necessarily capture what he did with his hands. I depended on my lawyer to address both.

   **His closing.** His closing used a broad political argument against government power.

   **A new proposal while the jury was still deciding.** A resolution had already been discussed before I paid the additional $2,000. The particular reduced proposal pressed after the chambers conference was first presented to me during deliberations. I wanted the jury to finish. I repeatedly refused and told him to stop pressing. He kept trying to reverse my decision. On a courthouse bench, while I prayed, he scolded me and said, in substance, that he did not believe in “that religion stuff.” He continued pressing me to accept.

   **The consequence he did not explain.** He never explained that the resolution he was pressing would ordinarily close the route to a direct appeal over the prosecutor’s earlier conduct. He kept urging acceptance without explaining that cost, the alternatives, or the significance of the mock-pistol incident for my options. The decision to accept was mine, and I needed that information before deciding. He also did not tell me I could bring the prosecutor’s conduct to a disciplinary authority. What did he do about reporting conduct he had personally witnessed?

   **The call afterward.** He first told me to expect further proceedings. About a day later he called, sounding pleased, and said Kanemoto was “spooked” and would not proceed further. What prompted that description, and what had the lawyers discussed after the mock-pistol incident?

10. <span id="account-22" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**The unidentified prosecution source — A proposal that I leave Hawaiʻi.** Stanley told me the departure proposal came from the prosecutor. It centered on my departure while the Hartmanns were separately pressing me to leave and I had reported their death threat to counsel.

    I received no written terms, identification of the originating prosecutor, or clear explanation of whether this was a formal offer or an informal idea. Who originated the proposal, and what did that person communicate?

## III. Assault, stalking, vehicle attacks, and the disputed sworn denial

11. <span id="account-10" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**The redacted defendant and sworn witness — Assault, repeated vehicle threats, and an attempt to run me down.** I ended a brief work relationship and social acquaintance with this man in September 2021. He owed me approximately $200 for work, which I sought by text and email. He never paid it. He stalked and harassed me for approximately a year.

    **The incidents included:**

    - **December 2021, Malama Marketplace:** He threatened or intimidated me with his vehicle.
    - **January 2022, Starbucks:** He physically assaulted me. HPD investigated and referred the assault to prosecutors. My original report placed it in November 2021; a contemporaneous photo I found after the proceedings established January 2022. After the assault I wrote off the unpaid debt and said I would avoid further in-person contact.
    - **June 2022, Breakers / North Shore Marketplace:** Another vehicle threat.
    - **July 2022, Waialua Beach Road:** Vehicle intimidation while I was cycling. I sent a cease-and-desist email on July 24. The harassment continued.
    - **September 12, 2022, PaaLaa Road:** I was walking east on the left side of the road. He accelerated from behind with the engine redlining, crossed the double yellow line toward me, narrowly missed me, returned to the travel lane, and raced away. I recognized the stick-shift vehicle because I had ridden in and driven it. I reported the attempt to run me down under **HPD report 22-353421**.
    - **September 19–22, 2022:** Continued harassment after I submitted a restraining order at Wahiawa Police Station on September 16. **Report 22-365099** concerns the September 20 Breakers encounter. He also filed a restraining order and continued harassing me during the competing proceedings, including taunts about my deceased parents: “How’s your parents?”

    In 2021 he had received a Schedule I substance from a woman inside Stonefish Grill and then furnished it to me. A separate transfer took place in his vehicle in the restaurant’s parking lot, described below. I reported the distribution through the DEA and HPD Narcotics/Vice before the December 2022 hearing and again to HPD afterward, identifying Stonefish Grill as a potential source of surveillance footage. No investigative action was communicated to me through those reports.

    A few days before the hearing, I overheard him say he had “another buddy who is federal.”

    At the December 2 hearing, he denied furnishing the substance immediately after Loo’s visual signal. I knew that denial was false from the transfer I had witnessed. During final remarks, after testimony had closed, he repeated another factual claim I dispute as false, and I was prevented from answering it.

12. <span id="account-11" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**The unidentified woman inside Stonefish Grill — The first transfer.** In 2021, inside Stonefish Grill in Haleʻiwa, I saw a woman supply the substance to the man identified above. He then furnished it to me. I gave law enforcement the location and the participants’ conduct. I identified restaurant surveillance as a potential record of the exchange.

13. <span id="account-12" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**The unidentified man in the Stonefish Grill parking lot — Approximately 100 tabs in the passenger seat.** During a separate encounter in the restaurant’s parking lot, I sat in the back seat of the defendant’s vehicle. A man in the front passenger seat displayed approximately 100 tabs and furnished one to me. I reported both transfers. The defendant’s sworn denial concerned his furnishing the substance to me inside Stonefish Grill.

## IV. The judge’s signal, the interrupted record, and the sealing request

14. <span id="account-13" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Judge Wilson M.N. Loo](https://disclosures.civilbeat.org/disclosures/wilson-loo-2-2/) — Signaling “no” to a sworn witness, then stopping me from describing it.** At the December 2, 2022 hearing in Honolulu, I represented myself and sought protection from the assault, stalking, harassment, and vehicle attacks. I arrived with a stack of documents. When I tried to explain the incidents, Loo cut me off and said, in substance, that I could go on all day. He did not ask me for a short account or let me finish presenting the conduct for which I sought protection.

    When the sworn witness was asked whether he had furnished the substance, I saw Loo turn toward him and signal “no” with his head and facial expression. The movement included a nose-scrunching, lip-pursing expression. I saw the witness and his lawyer, Bosko Petricevic, looking toward Loo. The witness then denied the transfer.

    I immediately began: “Let the record show that the judge just—” Loo cut me off, shouting, “Nah ah ah enough out of you!!”

    The hearing was recorded by audio only. Loo’s gesture was therefore outside the recording, and he stopped my immediate attempt to describe it aloud.

    Loo later allowed the opposing party to repeat a false claim during final remarks without allowing my rebuttal. He denied my requested injunction and granted Petricevic’s request to seal the court record. That restricted outside access to the spoken proceedings and the file. Loo had himself served on the Commission on Judicial Conduct. How does he explain his actions given that oversight experience?

15. <span id="account-14" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Bosko Petricevic](https://boskolaw.com/meet-your-attorney/) — Facing the judge during the signal, then requesting sealing.** Petricevic represented the defendant. Before the hearing, he offered and urged a cross-injunction resolution that would have avoided live testimony. I did not accept it.

    During the disputed testimony, he sat facing the bench. I personally saw him looking toward Loo as Loo gave the signal to his client. His client then denied the transfer. During final remarks the client repeated another false claim after testimony had closed; I was prevented from responding.

    Petricevic requested sealing, and Loo granted it. The result restricted public access to the spoken sequence, my interrupted record statement, and the hearing file. What did Petricevic see and understand, why did he seek sealing, and did he [report the judge’s conduct to an appropriate authority](https://www.courts.state.hi.us/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/hrpcond_ada.htm#TOC1_72 "Hawaiʻi Rules of Professional Conduct, Rule 8.3(b)")? I have received no account from him answering those questions.

## V. Police reports blocked, complaints diverted, and review closed

### Spring 2021: the Malama Market encounters

16. <span id="account-20" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[The HPD officers at Malama Market](https://www.honolulupd.org/) — Repeated tickets and a cruiser accelerating toward me.** Just after my spring 2021 FBI contact, an officer repeatedly pinned parking tickets on my truck at Malama Market in Haleʻiwa. Another HPD cruiser accelerated up to me in the parking lot. I regarded those actions as intimidation.

### The September 2022 vehicle reports and subsequent police responses

17. <span id="account-15" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Officer Brandt](https://www.honolulupd.org/) — Contradictory service information and a later blocked death-threat report.** [Officer Shatoo](https://www.honolulupd.org/) told me the restraining order had been served. During the continued September 2022 harassment, Brandt gave me contrary information about that same service status. I identify Brandt as the responding officer at the September 20 Breakers / North Shore Marketplace encounter, **report 22-365099**, and Shatoo with the September 12 PaaLaa Road report, **22-353421**.

    In a separate encounter after 2022, I tried to report the Hartmann death threat to another HPD officer. Brandt prevented that officer from receiving it. The original threat had already been dismissed by my assigned lawyer; Brandt later blocked this attempt to place it into a police record.

18. <span id="account-18" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[The HPD detective](https://www.honolulupd.org/) — Taxes on money I had never received.** In a phone call I recall as following the September 12, 2022 vehicle attack, an HPD detective brought up taxes in connection with the approximately $200 I was owed for work. The man I had reported had never paid me. I was reporting an attempt to run me down; the detective turned the conversation to taxes on money I had never received.

    Who was the detective, and how does HPD explain that response to my vehicle-attack report?

19. <span id="account-19" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[The HPD desk sergeant](https://www.honolulupd.org/) — Acknowledging my report, then offering a hypothetical contrary witness.** Before the December 2022 hearing, a desk sergeant told me, in substance, that according to my report there was something there. He then suggested that there might be other information. When I asked what kind, he offered the example of someone having seen something differently. He identified no actual witness, report, or conflicting observation.

    My report described acceleration from behind, a vehicle crossing the double yellow toward me, a near miss, and the driver racing away. If HPD had contrary evidence, what was it and how was it handled? A hypothetical witness did not answer the attack I reported.

### Refused reports and the later death-threat reporting attempt

20. <span id="account-16" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[The female HPD officer reached through 911](https://www.honolulupd.org/) — Refusing two complaints.** After the December 2022 hearing, I called 911 to report the false sworn denial. The officer told me, in substance, that proving perjury was my responsibility rather than HPD’s responsibility to investigate. I then raised the substance distribution as a separate complaint and identified Stonefish Grill, the people involved, and potential surveillance footage. She still refused to take that report.

21. <span id="account-17" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[The HPD officer](https://www.honolulupd.org/) who said a public figure’s circle was allowed to make murder threats.** During the later threat-reporting encounter, an officer told me, in substance, that members of an unnamed public figure’s circle were allowed to make murder threats. That statement is part of my complaint about the police response.

22. <span id="account-21" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Additional HPD personnel](https://www.honolulupd.org/) — Complaints dismissed, evidence locations supplied, and no overall resolution communicated.** I reported assault, stalking, repeated vehicle threats, the PaaLaa Road attack, substance distribution, and the false sworn denial. Officers dismissed or declined reports, and I received no follow-up about specific evidence locations I supplied. HPD advised me to avoid PaaLaa Road. I encountered the man while walking or cycling on main roads in Haleʻiwa.

    HPD did investigate the Starbucks assault and refer it to prosecutors. Other complaints were refused, and the handling of other reports was never explained to me. None resulted in prosecution of the defendant. I received no explanation of how HPD handled the other reports. HPD personnel also responded to reports about Loo by calling him “honorable.” His title did not answer what I reported seeing him do.

### The assault referral and institutional review

23. <span id="account-23" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**The charging and reviewing prosecutors — No prosecution after the assault referral.** HPD investigated the Starbucks assault and referred it to prosecutors. No prosecution followed that referral or my reports of stalking, harassment, vehicle threats, and the PaaLaa Road attack. Who reviewed the assault referral, what decision was made and why, and what reached prosecutors concerning the subsequent conduct?

24. <span id="account-24" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**The Commission on Judicial Conduct and [Chair Dickson C.H. Lee](https://www.martindale.com/attorney/dickson-c-h-lee-892806/) — “Insufficient evidence,” then “no jurisdiction.”** I contacted the Commission immediately after the December 2, 2022 hearing. Its March 13, 2025 letter, signed by Chair Dickson C.H. Lee, says an earlier complaint was closed for “insufficient evidence” on March 22, 2023. The letter does not identify what primary evidence the Commission examined for that decision.

    The same letter declined renewed review because Loo was “no longer a per diem judge as of July 2024” and more than 90 days had passed since his departure. My original contact and the reported 2023 closure both preceded that departure. What did the Commission do with my timely report? Did it obtain the hearing audio, interview people present, examine the interrupted record statement, or investigate the visual signal?

    The Judiciary’s public website still listed Loo as an active First Circuit Per Diem Judge in May 2025, after the Commission’s letter said he had left in July 2024. His name was later removed. That discrepancy needs an explanation. I received an insufficient-evidence disposition followed by a jurisdictional refusal, without an account of how the primary evidence had been examined.

25. <span id="account-25" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**The Hawaiʻi Ethics Commission — Confusion over its authority when I sought review.** When I asked the Ethics Commission about the judicial-conduct issues, it expressed confusion over its own authority. That response left me without a clear review path through that office. It belongs alongside the Commission on Judicial Conduct’s closures as part of what happened when I sought institutional scrutiny.

## What happened to my complaints {#case-outcomes}

I reported a death threat to my lawyers; it was dismissed and left out of my testimony. I tried to report it to police; an officer blocked the report. I tried to describe a judge’s visual signal for an audio-only record; the judge stopped me, and the proceedings were then sealed.

These failures accumulate: what never enters the record becomes harder to bring before the next reviewer.

My complaints produced no prosecution of the man I reported for assault, stalking, and the vehicle attacks. The December 2, 2022 hearing record was sealed. The Commission on Judicial Conduct reported its 2023 closure and refused renewed review in 2025.

I submitted an online FBI report in spring 2021 and later met with the FBI in Mokuleia about earlier threats and law-enforcement conduct. I received no communicated investigative disposition or protective action specifically addressing the Hartmann threat. The DOJ Public Integrity Section acknowledged receipt of my July 12, 2025 submission. No decision addressing the substance of that submission has been communicated to me.
