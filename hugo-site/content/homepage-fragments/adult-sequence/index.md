Gene Hartmann told me, “Stay away from [name withheld].” Rita Hartmann added, “Or you will get whacked.” When I reported the threat to my assigned lawyer, Audrey Stanley, she called it a “conditional death threat” and dismissed it, in substance, as “no big deal.” Years later, Officer Brandt prevented another officer from receiving my report of the same threat.

I reported an attempt to run me down, and an HPD detective asked whether I would pay taxes on approximately $200 the man I reported had never paid me. I went to court seeking protection. My cycling on a public path was portrayed as stalking even though the other man approached me from behind. The judge denied my requested injunction and sealed the proceedings at the other lawyer’s request.

I name the people involved and describe what I saw and heard. The dates, report numbers, and outcomes are here so that each person and institution can answer for the conduct attributed to them.

## I. Death threats and pressure to stay silent

1. <span id="account-3" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Gene (Eugene) Hartmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Security_Agency) — “Stay away,” then a “murder mystery.”** I knew Gene and Rita Hartmann through the Haleʻiwa duplex where I rented the back unit and they occupied the front. During an in-person encounter after February 2016 and before July 2017, Gene calmly told me, “Stay away from [name withheld].” Rita immediately supplied the consequence: “Or you will get whacked.”

   I was investigating matters for my defense and intended to disclose what I learned at trial. Gene knew about my investigation. The following day, Gene recast the exchange as a “murder mystery.” I told him I would tell everyone. Gene also pressed me to leave Hawaiʻi.

   Before that encounter, a man in Aiea had also told me to stay away from the same person. I can identify him by sight. I had been asking questions in a local technical community I helped establish. The repeated instruction and Gene and Rita’s delivery led me to believe their threat had been prepared in advance. I want the speakers questioned about who they had spoken with, what they knew of my inquiries, and whether they had discussed the warning before delivering it.

2. <span id="account-4" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**Rita Hartmann — “Or you will get whacked.”** Rita delivered the death threat that completed Gene’s instruction to stay away. She spoke calmly and quietly during the same encounter. Her words made the consequence explicit: death if I did not comply.

3. <span id="account-5" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**“Kevin” — A warning to stop talking, backed by a threat to destroy my career.** In May or June 2017, a man I identify as Kevin approached me outside Kahala Whole Foods. He described himself as an associate of the public figure named in the earlier threat and that person’s spouse. He threatened to destroy my career if I continued discussing the Hartmann threat and related conduct.

   Kevin also invoked a prominent technology investor’s name to reinforce the warning. The encounter came shortly before I would testify in July 2017.

## II. The tax-office account, the imaginary gun, and my lawyers

The 2015–2017 case began with the November 2015 tax-office encounter and was pending when the Hartmanns threatened me. It went to trial in July 2017. The threat, the advice to omit it from my testimony, and the prosecutor’s closing all belong to that sequence.

4. <span id="account-1" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Young Kwak](https://tax.hawaii.gov/) — A demand for seven times the tax owed, followed by false accounts of the encounter.** In November 2015, Kwak verbally attacked and aggressively pressured me after the door closed on a tiny, enclosed tax-office booth. He demanded approximately seven times what I owed. I told him the amount I owed; the final calculation came in just below the figure I had given him. He threatened that leaving would mean dealing with someone who “wasn’t nice.”

   Kwak subsequently gave the grand jury a false accusation about what I had said. I heard that accusation in the grand-jury recording. My words concerned exposing what was happening and telling everyone. At trial, the earlier accusation was not presented to the jury. Kwak instead testified, in substance, that he merely told me which form to fill out. That exchange did not happen. His routine-form account left out the aggressive pressure and gave the jury a false picture of his conduct. His description of that encounter was central to what the jury was being asked to believe.

   The diagram presented to the jury made the enclosed booth appear substantially larger and more comfortable than it was. The jury saw that depiction without the photograph of the actual booth I had asked my lawyer to obtain. The form story and the diagram reinforced the same false picture: routine instructions in a roomy setting, rather than aggressive pressure inside a tiny closed booth.

   [![Tax-office cartoon showing a man in a confined booth facing a shouting collector labeled Y. Kwak, beside a checklist of seven times the tax owed, false testimony, and a misleading booth diagram.](/img/homepage-tax-office.jpg)](https://tax.hawaii.gov/)

5. <span id="account-2" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[A man introduced to me as a New Jersey State Police investigator](https://www.njsp.gov/) — The “colonoscopy” warning.** In an encounter I place in November 2015, after the tax-office dispute, a man introduced to me as a New Jersey State Police organized-crime-task-force investigator demonstrated knowledge of people I had known growing up and invoked what he described as their organized-crime associations. He told me he would give me a “colonoscopy.” James Yuen stood beside him.

   He [invoked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethals_Bridge) my personal history while delivering that warning during questioning about the tax-office matter.

6. <span id="account-9" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[James Yuen](https://openpayrolls.com/employee/james-yuen-9557) — Beside the investigator during the threat, then preparing the case with Kanemoto.** Yuen stood beside the man who invoked my childhood associates and delivered the “colonoscopy” warning. Yuen handled the tax investigation. I later personally observed him working directly with prosecutor Vincent Kanemoto in trial preparation; that preparation also involved Kwak.

   What did Yuen tell or send Kanemoto about the November 2015 encounter or people I knew growing up? When did Kwak’s routine-form account first appear, who participated in preparing it, and what notes or messages record that sequence?

7. <span id="account-6" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Vincent Kanemoto](https://ag.hawaii.gov/) — Pointing an imaginary gun at the jury and urging conviction.** During closing argument in July 2017, Kanemoto acknowledged that no gun was involved. He then formed both hands into a mock pistol, pointed it toward all twelve jurors, and asked them to imagine a gun anyway. His closing words were, in substance: “There was no gun in this trial. But what if there was? Convict him. I rest my case.”

   The judge immediately called a recess and brought the lawyers into chambers. I also recall an instruction to disregard the episode.

   Kanemoto’s presentation omitted Kwak’s earlier false accusation and the recordings of my distress, while putting forward Kwak’s false routine-form account and the misleading booth diagram. Yuen worked directly with him preparing the case. The actual death threat I had reported to my lawyers did not reach the jury through my testimony; Kanemoto’s imaginary weapon did reach the jury through his closing.

   When I confronted Kanemoto at Glazer’s in Haleʻiwa in an encounter I place in summer 2019, he denied making the mock-pistol gesture.

8. <span id="account-7" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Audrey L.E. Stanley](https://lawyers.findlaw.com/hawaii/honolulu/5063522_1/) — Dismissing the death threat and relaying a proposal that I leave Hawaiʻi.** Stanley was my assigned lawyer when I reported the Hartmann threat. I began explaining who had spoken, the condition attached to the threat, and why I took it seriously. She interrupted, called it a “conditional death threat,” and dismissed it, in substance, as “no big deal.” She did not explain how it would be documented, investigated, or addressed, or what reporting and protective options were available.

   Separately, while reviewing the grand-jury account of my distress during the tax-office encounter, she laughed.

   Not long after my threat report, Stanley relayed a proposal originating with the prosecutor that centered on my departure from Hawaiʻi. She supplied no written terms or clear explanation.

   Stanley’s [2024 judicial financial disclosure](https://www.courts.state.hi.us/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/StanleyAudrey2024FDS.pdf#page=1) gives September 23, 2024 as the date of her appointment as a per diem Family Court judge. The conduct described here concerns her earlier representation of me.

9. <span id="account-8" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**Unnamed retained lawyer — Advising me to omit the death threat, leaving evidence unchallenged, and pressing a resolution I refused.**

   **The death-threat report and my testimony.** I personally told him that Gene and Rita Hartmann had threatened to kill me. He did not take it seriously, did not explain investigative or protective options, and advised me to leave the death threat out of my testimony. I followed his advice. The jury did not hear that account from me. Kevin’s warning to stop talking was also still affecting my willingness to disclose the threat.

   **The box seat at the 2016 Eddie.** My lawyer told me he attended the [2016 Eddie competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eddie) and was in the box seat with the person he called “the high priest.” This was the lawyer to whom I reported Gene and Rita Hartmann’s death threat, and who advised me to leave it out of my testimony.

   **The fees and the additional work.** His stated fees were $6,000 for a negotiated resolution and $8,000 for a full trial. I paid the additional $2,000 because I wanted a jury trial. He repeatedly pressed me to accept a resolution despite my refusals. Did the fee arrangement affect his advice or his decisions about seeking corrective remedies, and if so, how?

   **“Tight trial,” “looking guilty,” and “Maybe he did it.”** Before the proceedings, counsel told me the judge wanted a “tight trial.” During preparation, counsel said he was counting on me “looking guilty.” He opened with words to the effect of “Maybe he did it.” Those were my lawyer’s words about me, not my statements. He introduced that possibility before testing the witnesses. His pressure to accept a resolution had already begun before the mock-pistol incident and continued despite my repeated refusals.

   **Kwak’s testimony and my immediate objection.** His cross-examination of Kwak was cursory. I immediately told him that Kwak’s routine-form testimony was false and that the exchange Kwak described had never happened. He dismissed the point, in substance, as immaterial and not perjury. That answer did not address the false picture being given to the jury or Kwak’s credibility. My complaint was about a witness describing an exchange that did not happen. Did counsel compare Kwak’s trial account with his earlier statements or the grand-jury recording? What steps did he take to test the form detail, challenge the account, or correct the picture presented to the jury? What was recorded?

He did not obtain the photograph. The jury saw a diagram that made the booth substantially larger and more comfortable than it was. He did not adequately challenge that depiction. The photograph I requested would have given the jury a direct comparison with the place where the encounter occurred.

   **What he failed to explain after chambers.** My lawyer did not adequately explain what happened in chambers immediately after Kanemoto’s mock-pistol closing, whether the instruction to disregard the episode was sufficient, or the alternatives to continuing before the same jury. No mistrial motion was made in my presence. The questions included an objection, a mistrial request, further corrective instructions, describing the gesture on the record, and preserving the incident for later challenge. A transcript of the prosecutor’s words would not necessarily capture what he did with his hands. I depended on my lawyer to address both.

   **His closing.** His closing used a broad political argument against government power.

   **Pressure during jury deliberations.** After the mock-pistol closing and chambers conference, while the jury deliberated, my lawyer presented a reduced proposal to me for the first time. I wanted the jury to finish. I repeatedly refused and told him to stop pressing. He kept trying to reverse my decision. On a courthouse bench, while I prayed, he scolded me and said, in substance, that I did not believe in “that religion stuff.” He continued pressing me to accept.

   **The consequence he did not explain.** He did not explain that accepting an ordinary unconditional resolution could foreclose a direct appeal over the prosecutor’s earlier conduct. He kept urging acceptance without explaining that cost, the alternatives, or the significance of the mock-pistol incident for my options. I repeatedly refused the proposal. He also did not tell me I could report the prosecutor’s conduct to a disciplinary authority. What did he do to evaluate or report the prosecutorial conduct he personally witnessed, including under HRPC Rule 8.3(a)?

   **The call afterward.** My lawyer first told me to expect another trial. About a day later he called, sounding pleased, and said Kanemoto was “spooked” and would not proceed further. What prompted that description, and what had the lawyers discussed after the mock-pistol incident?

## IV. The judge’s signal, the interrupted record, and the sealing request

14. <span id="account-13" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Judge Wilson M.N. Loo](https://disclosures.civilbeat.org/disclosures/wilson-loo-2-2/) — Signaling “no” to a sworn witness, then stopping me from describing it.** At the December 2, 2022 hearing in Honolulu, I represented myself and sought protection from the assault, stalking, harassment, and vehicle attacks. I arrived with a stack of documents. When I tried to explain the incidents, Loo cut me off and said, in substance, that I could go on all day. He did not ask me for a short account or let me finish presenting the conduct for which I sought protection.

    Before the disputed question, I had submitted a text-message exhibit relevant to the transfer being addressed in court. It supplied context for my question about the witness’s conduct. The exhibit remains in the sealed court file.

    When the sworn witness was asked whether he had furnished the substance, I saw Loo turn toward him and signal “no” with his head and facial expression. The movement included a nose-scrunching, lip-pursing expression. I saw the witness and his lawyer, Bosko Petricevic, looking toward Loo. The witness then denied the transfer.

    I immediately began: “Let the record show that the judge just—” Loo cut me off, shouting, “Nah ah ah enough out of you!!”

    The hearing was recorded by audio only. Loo’s gesture was therefore outside the recording, and he stopped my immediate attempt to describe it aloud.

    Loo later allowed the opposing party to address the court during final remarks without allowing me to respond to his account. He denied my requested injunction; the opposing party obtained an injunction, and Loo granted Petricevic’s request to seal the court record. Sealing restricted outside access to the spoken proceedings and the file. Loo had himself served on the Commission on Judicial Conduct. Why did he stop my immediate attempt to preserve the movement in an audio-only proceeding, and what did he understand about the effect of sealing on access to the recording and exhibit?

    [Rutgers Law’s August 2024 alumni account](https://law.rutgers.edu/alumni-updates-1) identifies Loo as a 1980 graduate and a former organized-crime prosecutor for Honolulu. His professional experience makes his handling of the evidence and my attempt to preserve the signal a question he should answer directly.

15. <span id="account-14" class="home-account-anchor"></span>**[Bosko Petricevic](https://boskolaw.com/meet-your-attorney/) — Portraying my presence on a public road as stalking, then requesting sealing.** Petricevic represented the defendant. Before the hearing, he offered and urged a cross-injunction resolution that would have avoided live testimony. I did not accept it.

    At the hearing, Petricevic used the final, post-service Haleʻiwa Road encounter to portray me as stalking his client. I was cycling on the ocean-side public path. His client came from behind in a vehicle, slowed beside me, and growled. I called 911. I was not following or approaching him. I was not given a chance to answer counsel’s portrayal of my travel on that public path as stalking.

    During the disputed testimony, he sat facing the bench. I personally saw him looking toward Loo as Loo gave the signal to his client. His client then denied the transfer. After testimony had closed, his client addressed the court during final remarks; I was prevented from responding.

    Petricevic’s [résumé filed with the Hawaiʻi Labor Relations Board](https://labor.hawaii.gov/hlrb/files/2024/01/Bosko-Petricevic-RESUME-2024.pdf) places him in the Attorney General’s Employment Law Division from June 2013 to March 2019, encompassing the 2015–2017 case. An [official 2016 filing](https://ag.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/News-Release-2016-77.pdf) identifies Kanemoto as a Deputy Attorney General during that period. His Attorney General service overlapped with Kanemoto’s work on the 2015–2017 case. Did they discuss me or exchange information later available to Petricevic when he represented the opposing party before Loo?

    Petricevic requested sealing, and Loo granted it. The result restricted public access to the spoken sequence, my interrupted record statement, and the hearing file. What did Petricevic see and understand, what action did he take to preserve or report the alleged judicial signal under [HRPC Rule 8.3(b)](https://www.courts.state.hi.us/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/hrpcond_ada.htm), and why did he request sealing? I have received no account from him answering those questions.

## What did officials know about me?
