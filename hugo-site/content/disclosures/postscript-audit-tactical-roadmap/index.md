### I. The Context of the Archive

*Oahu Underground* (available at [gtcode.com](https://gtcode.com)) is an independent, public-interest records-audit project based in Hawaii. In the archive, I document institutional accountability gaps, closed-loop oversight structures, and network overlaps within Hawaii's judiciary, executive branch, and media ecosystem. The database is built on the principle of **procedural minimalism and active retrieval**—the assertion that systemic opacity can only be tested by analyzing primary sources, corporate filings, and court records.

If the primary investigator becomes unavailable, incapacitated, deceased, or otherwise unable to continue the archive, the project faces an immediate operational transition. Secondary reviewers need to preserve the files, maintain retrieval paths, and establish what review has occurred. Loss of the archive's steward creates a continuity problem; it does not establish how any institution will respond.

This document identifies failure modes to watch for during that transition and protocols for preserving an inspectable record. Each failure mode requires evidence of the actual response. The purpose is to keep the audit functional, not to treat criticism or silence as confirmation of its claims.

---

### II. Review Risks After the Investigator's Departure

Secondary reviewers should distinguish a recorded disposition from examination of the underlying claim. Two failure modes warrant particular attention:

*   **Procedural Closure Without Merits Review:** A file may be closed for an administrative or jurisdictional reason without resolving the evidence. Preserve the stated reason, the applicable process, and any record of what was examined. A procedural closure establishes a disposition; it does not by itself establish substantive review or deliberate avoidance.
*   **Biography Substituted for Evidence Review:** A characterization of the investigator may replace examination of a particular claim. The indicator is the review itself: what statement was challenged, what evidence was considered, and what reason was given? A personal label is not an answer to a document or firsthand report. Criticism that identifies contradictions or evidentiary defects is different from dismissal that leaves the claim unexamined.

---

### III. Biography and Record Review

Concern about my wellbeing, disagreement with my interpretation, and fair examination of the record can coexist. Evidence-supported challenges to credibility and reasoning are legitimate parts of review.

The failure occurs when a characterization of me substitutes for examining a specific claim and its evidence. That substitution leaves the claim unexamined; it does not establish its truth or the reviewer's motive. A reviewer can confirm, contradict, narrow, or leave a claim unresolved after examining its evidence.

Secondary reviewers should identify the claim, retain its existing evidence class, examine supporting material and corroboration or contradiction, and record the basis for their conclusion. Firsthand accounts remain firsthand evidence; independent corroboration is a separate question. Biographical context matters only where its relevance to a specific evidentiary question is explained.

### IV. Continuity Failure Modes

The following are risks to investigate if the archive loses its active steward. Their presence and causes must be established from records of preservation, access, follow-up, and review.

#### 1. Non-Response and Loss of Follow-Up

Without an active steward to preserve files, pursue requests, and track dispositions, a matter can remain unaddressed. The practical consequence is continued non-review, whether the cause is inattention, workload, jurisdictional limits, or deliberate avoidance. Nonresponse alone does not distinguish those explanations.

A successor should preserve what was submitted, to whom, when, any documented receipt, and the response or absence of one. An unanswered publication, a received request, and an express refusal are different records. “No response received” should not become “refused to answer” without evidence of refusal. The status of the underlying claim remains a separate evidence question.

#### 2. Sealed Records and the Limits of Audio

The hearing documented in [The Nod](/hawaii-courts/the-nod-visual-allegation/#the-courtroom-sequence) illustrates why a successor must pursue visual corroboration and audio-record access as separate retrieval tasks.

A successor should identify the existing recording, any transcript, the sealing order, and the available access process. Access requirements must be checked against those records rather than assumed from whether the reviewer was a party. Independent corroboration of the visual account requires a separate source, such as another observer or a visual recording if one exists. [The Nod](/hawaii-courts/the-nod-visual-allegation/) separates the visual signal from the audio-confirmable sequence.

#### 3. Access Restrictions and Professional Friction

Withheld documents, declined interviews, and exclusion from relevant channels can obstruct a successor's review. Record the specific request, response, restriction, and stated reason. Do not infer a common source or shared purpose from several access problems alone; any claimed connection requires its own evidence.

### V. Operational Protocols: Preservation, Review, and Publication

To prevent the archive from becoming an inactive record, secondary reviewers should preserve the evidence classes and execute targeted, records-first protocols across three distinct lanes:

#### 1. Preservation Protocol
*   **Mirrors and Repositories:** Maintain decentralized copies of the archive.
    *   *Git repository mirror:* [GTCode Press](https://github.com/GTCode-Press/publications) (to host the source code, documents, and revision history).
    *   *Wayback Machine Cataloging:* Ensure systematic crawl requests of [gtcode.com](https://gtcode.com) are registered with the Internet Archive.
*   **Backups and Hashes:** Generate and publish SHA-256 checksums for all raw PDFs, court audio recordings, and database CSV exports to support tamper detection and version verification.
*   **Digital Custodianship:** Maintain an independent digital custodian to hold backup decryption keys and manage domain registration transitions.

#### 2. Review Protocol
*   **Claims Matrix:** Map all allegations to their corresponding primary sources, distinguishing verified records from interpretive claims.
*   **Sourcing Discipline:** Every quantitative and structural assertion must be tied to a specific public record:
    *   *Commission on Judicial Conduct (CJC) Metrics:* FY 2024–2025 reports 1,355 inquiries, 11 complaints processed, 6 dismissed, and 5 pending at year-end. [^1]
    *   *Campaign Finance & Donor Networks:* Individual and corporate candidate contributions. [^2]
    *   *IRS Form 990 Filings:* Financial structures of related civic organizations and foundations. [^3]
    *   *Corporate Registrations:* Tracing corporate/business interests associated with relevant persons or spouses for cross-checking against financial disclosures. [^4]

#### 3. Publication Protocol
*   **Republishing and Attribution:** Safely republish public records and audited statistics. Any claims regarding unverified or sealed proceedings must be explicitly attributed as allegations or inferences, rather than presented as established facts.
*   **Legal Review:** Perform a pre-publication legal review for compliance with defamation and privacy laws before releasing editorialized summaries or narrative connections.
